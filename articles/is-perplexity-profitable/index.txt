21:T2e9f,<h2 id="short-answer">Short Answer</h2><p><strong>Perplexity has not disclosed a verified public profit figure as of November 10, 2026.</strong> Recent reporting puts its annualized revenue above $750 million, but that is a run-rate estimate. It does not show whether the company made a net profit after paying for model inference, cloud infrastructure, research, staff and other operating costs.</p>
<p>The latest direct reporting on profitability that we found is a Financial Times report from April 2026. It said Perplexity remained loss-making because of model and inference costs. A newer November 10 review of large AI companies still lists Perplexity as having no disclosed profit figure. The careful answer is therefore not &quot;Perplexity is profitable&quot; or &quot;Perplexity definitely loses money today.&quot; It is <strong>profitability has not been publicly confirmed</strong>.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-question-really-means">What the Question Really Means</h2><p>People often use revenue, annual recurring revenue, gross margin and profit as if they mean the same thing. They do not.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Revenue</strong> is the money a business earns from customers over a period.</li>
<li><strong>Annual recurring revenue (ARR)</strong> is a run-rate measure that annualizes recurring revenue at a point in time.</li>
<li><strong>Gross margin</strong> compares revenue with the direct cost of delivering the product.</li>
<li><strong>Operating profit</strong> subtracts operating expenses such as salaries, research, sales and administration.</li>
<li><strong>Net income</strong> includes additional items such as interest and taxes.</li>
<li><strong>Cash flow</strong> tracks money entering and leaving the business.</li>
</ul>
<p>A company can have fast-growing ARR and still lose money. It can also have positive gross margin while spending more than it earns on research, infrastructure and hiring. To answer whether Perplexity is profitable, we need a disclosed profit measure, not just a revenue headline.</p>
<h2 id="how-it-works">How It Works</h2><h3 id="what-public-revenue-reports-show">What public revenue reports show</h3><p>A November 2026 review by <a href="https://theaireceipts.com/blog/which-ai-companies-are-profitable/">The AI Receipts</a> says Perplexity had more than $750 million in annualized revenue but had not disclosed a profit figure. The review says its data was checked on November 9 and 10, 2026, and separates reported revenue from profit claims.</p>
<p>Reuters reported in August 2026 that Perplexity&#39;s annualized revenue had risen above $750 million, citing The Information. That report also described Perplexity Computer as one source of growth. The figure is useful context, but it is still a reported run rate, not a public income statement.</p>
<p>The Financial Times reported in April 2026 that Perplexity&#39;s estimated ARR had passed $450 million in March, a 50% month-on-month rise. The same report said the company remained loss-making because of model and inference costs. That is the latest direct report of losses we found, but it is not a current audited result for November.</p>
<h3 id="why-ai-search-has-meaningful-costs">Why AI search has meaningful costs</h3><p>Perplexity&#39;s product answers questions by retrieving information and using AI models to produce responses. Each query can require search infrastructure, model inference, storage, ranking, safety checks and supporting systems. More complex agent tasks can require several model calls and additional tools.</p>
<p>The cost can vary by user and task. A short factual question may use fewer resources than a long research request or a computer-use workflow. Paid subscriptions and usage-based credits can help manage the relationship between revenue and compute, but they do not prove that every customer segment is profitable.</p>
<p>The company has not published a full public cost breakdown. That means readers cannot calculate the exact cost of a query or determine how much of its reported revenue remains after compute and other expenses.</p>
<h3 id="why-private-company-figures-need-care">Why private-company figures need care</h3><p>Perplexity is a private company. Unlike a listed company, it does not have to publish quarterly earnings statements for the general public. Revenue and growth numbers may come from management comments, investors, people familiar with the company or independent research firms.</p>
<p>That does not make the numbers useless. It means readers need to label them correctly. A figure reported by a news outlet is not the same as an audited filing. A run-rate estimate is not the same as cash collected over a full financial year. A valuation is not profit.</p>
<h2 id="practical-steps">Practical Steps</h2><p>If you want to evaluate Perplexity&#39;s financial health, use a simple checklist.</p>
<p><strong>First, identify the metric and date.</strong> Is the figure annual revenue, monthly recurring revenue or ARR? Does it refer to March, August or a full fiscal year? Do not compare a March run rate with a full-year result without explaining the difference.</p>
<p><strong>Second, check the source.</strong> Prefer a company filing, an audited statement or a direct company statement. If a report cites anonymous sources, say so. If a research firm provides an estimate, label it as an estimate.</p>
<p><strong>Third, separate revenue from costs.</strong> Look for compute spending, cloud commitments, staff costs, research, marketing and customer support. A business can grow quickly while spending heavily to deliver each response.</p>
<p><strong>Fourth, ask what kind of margin is being discussed.</strong> Gross margin is not operating profit. A positive gross margin can still sit beside a large operating loss. Cash flow can also differ from accounting profit.</p>
<p><strong>Fifth, check whether the company has disclosed a profit number.</strong> If it has not, the correct public answer is &quot;not disclosed&quot; rather than a guess. Avoid turning a valuation or funding round into proof of profitability.</p>
<h2 id="common-problems">Common Problems</h2><p>The most common mistake is treating ARR as profit. If a company reports $750 million in annualized revenue, that does not mean it keeps $750 million. It must still pay for compute, employees, research, sales, legal work, offices and other costs.</p>
<p>Another mistake is using an old gross-margin estimate as if it were current net income. Gross margin may change as model providers, user mix and pricing change. It also excludes many expenses that matter for profitability.</p>
<p>A third problem is comparing estimates from different sources without checking their dates. One source may report revenue as of March, another may use an August run rate, and a third may discuss a target. Those numbers can all be true while describing different things.</p>
<p>Finally, the absence of a public profit figure is not proof that the company has no profit at all. It means outsiders cannot confirm the result from the public information available.</p>
<h2 id="what-does-not-work">What Does Not Work</h2><p>Do not conclude that Perplexity is profitable because its ARR is high, because investors value it highly or because it has a growing user base. Those facts may indicate commercial momentum, but they do not show net income.</p>
<p>Do not state that Perplexity is definitely unprofitable in November 2026 based only on an April report. That report is useful evidence of earlier losses, but it is not a fresh audited statement. The newer public review still says no profit figure has been disclosed.</p>
<p>Do not treat a funding round as operating revenue. Investment capital can fund growth, but it is not the same as income earned from customers. Keep financing, valuation and operating performance separate.</p>
<h2 id="safety-and-limitations">Safety and Limitations</h2><p>This article is a public-information summary, not investment advice. Private-company financial data is incomplete, and different reports may use different definitions. A company may share information with investors that is not available to the public.</p>
<p>The latest public sources we checked through November 10, 2026 do not provide a verified Perplexity profit figure. The April Financial Times report described the company as loss-making due to model and inference costs. Later revenue reports show growth, but they do not establish a change in profit status.</p>
<p>If Perplexity publishes audited statements or a direct profit disclosure, the answer should be updated. Until then, the most accurate description is that its profitability remains undisclosed publicly.</p>
