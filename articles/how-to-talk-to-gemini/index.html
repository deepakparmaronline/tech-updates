How to Talk to Gemini: Use Voice Chat and Gemini Live

Learn how to talk to Gemini with voice chat and Gemini Live on Android, including screen sharing, camera controls, privacy settings, and common fixes.

  • Google's current help page documents Live on Android phones and tablets and says it is not available in the Gemini web app.
  • Camera and screen sharing are optional and expose information from the device, so check the screen before enabling them.
  • Account, language, app version and gradual rollout can affect feature availability.

You can talk to Gemini in two ways: speak a prompt and receive a response, or start Gemini Live for a more natural back-and-forth conversation. Live is designed for speaking without typing, interrupting the assistant and continuing a conversation as you think. Google's current help instructions document Live on Android phones and tablets, with camera and screen-sharing controls on supported devices.

Short Answer

On a supported Android phone or tablet, open the Gemini mobile app and tap Live at the bottom of the screen, or swipe left to enter Live. Follow any first-time instructions and start speaking. You can also say "Hey Google, let's talk Live" or "Hey Google, let's talk" when the feature is available on your device.

Gemini Live is different from speaking one prompt into a microphone. Live keeps the conversation open so you can interrupt, ask a follow-up and switch between voice and text. Google says the feature is not currently available in the Gemini web app or Gemini in Google Messages. Availability can change as Google rolls out updates.

What the Question Really Means

A spoken prompt is useful when you want to dictate one question without typing. Gemini Live is designed for a longer conversation. You can explain an idea, stop Gemini while it is speaking, add context and change the subject without starting from a blank chat.

That difference matters for tasks such as practising for an interview, brainstorming a plan or asking for help while looking at something in front of you. Live can also use camera or screen sharing on supported Android devices. You should still treat it as an assistant, not as a person who can see everything accurately or understand every detail.

Google's Android Help page describes Gemini Live as a natural, free-flowing conversation with spoken replies. It also says you can interrupt Gemini and use it to explore a topic or practise aloud. The same page explains the controls for holding, muting, ending and resuming a session.

How It Works

Gemini voice input records a spoken prompt, while Gemini Live is designed for a continuing spoken conversation. Live sessions can be interrupted and resumed, and supported devices may offer camera or screen sharing controls. Availability depends on the device, account, language and rollout.

What You Need Before You Start

Google's current help page lists an Android phone or tablet, the Gemini mobile app or Gemini as your mobile assistant, the latest Google app and a signed-in Gemini Apps account. It says you must be over 13 or the applicable age in your country. A work or school Google account also needs access to Gemini Apps.

Some features are released gradually, so two people can see different controls even when they use the same app. Language availability can also vary. Google says the Gemini mobile app does not currently support every language and that Gems cannot be used with Gemini Live at the time of its current help page.

If you are using a managed work or school account, an administrator may control access to Gemini Apps and connected services. If the Live option is missing, check the account and app version before assuming that your phone is unsupported.

Practical Steps

Step 1: Open the Gemini mobile app

Unlock your Android phone or tablet and open the Gemini app. Sign in to the Google account that you want to use. If Gemini is set as your mobile assistant, you can also start Live with the voice phrase Google lists in its help page.

Step 2: Tap Live or swipe left

At the bottom of the Gemini app, tap the Live control. Google also documents swiping left to enter Live. The first time you use the feature, follow any on-screen setup instructions. The exact icons and layout can change as the app is updated.

Step 3: Start with a clear opening sentence

Tell Gemini what you want to discuss. For example, say, "Help me prepare for a job interview. Ask one question at a time and give feedback after each answer." A clear goal helps the conversation stay focused. If the answer is too broad, interrupt and narrow the task.

Step 4: Interrupt and add details

You can speak while Gemini is talking if you want to add information or change direction. If you prefer not to be interrupted, check the Live response setting. The feature is meant to support a natural conversation, but it can still misunderstand a word or lose a detail. Correct it when that happens.

How to Control a Live Session

Use Hold when you want to pause the microphone and return later. Use Mute when you want the microphone off while the Live session remains open. Google explains that a muted session can keep the video or screen share active, so check the screen before you leave the phone unattended.

Use End when you want to close the Live session and review the transcript. You can return to a previous Live chat from its conversation and tap Live again. Google also says you can switch between Live voice and non-Live text within the same conversation.

If you are using the phone while Live runs in the background, Google requires Live notifications to be enabled. You can return to the full-screen session from the Live with Gemini notification. You cannot start a Live session from a locked screen, and lock-screen behaviour depends on the Gemini lock-screen setting.

How to Share Your Camera or Screen

Show Gemini what your camera sees

During a Live chat, tap the camera control to share a camera view. This can help when you want to ask about a label, object or scene. You can switch between the front and rear camera and stop sharing when you are finished.

Google says the camera turns off if you put Live on hold, leave the Gemini app or lock the screen. It does not always turn itself back on when you return. Check the camera indicator before continuing so you know what is being shared.

Share your Android screen

Google's help page says screen sharing requires Gemini Live notifications to be enabled. Start the Live session, tap the screen-sharing control and follow the on-screen instructions. When you share the screen, your full screen is shared with Gemini, not just one app window.

To stop sharing, return to the Gemini app and turn it off, or use the Android screen-sharing notification. Google says screen sharing stops if Live is put on hold or the phone is locked. You need to start sharing again after the session resumes.

Before you share, close private messages, banking pages, password managers and confidential work documents. A notification that appears during screen sharing may expose information you did not intend to show. Do not use screen sharing when another person's private information is visible unless you have permission.

Change the Gemini Voice

If you want a different spoken voice, open Gemini settings and look for the voice setting. Google's help page says this setting applies to Gemini Live and other Gemini app features that respond verbally. The option may not be available in every language.

Changing the voice does not change the model or guarantee a different answer. It changes how Gemini sounds. If you want a different style of response, give Gemini an instruction in the conversation, such as asking it to speak more slowly or use shorter explanations.

Common Problems

If Live is missing, update the Google app and Gemini app, confirm that you are signed in and check whether your account is eligible. If you use a work or school account, ask the administrator whether Gemini Apps access is enabled. Some features are still rolling out and may not appear on every account.

If Gemini cannot hear you, check microphone permission and make sure another app is not using the microphone. If the conversation ends when you switch apps, enable the required notifications and check the background settings. If screen sharing stops, unlock the phone and start sharing again.

If Gemini answers the wrong question, repeat the key detail in a short sentence. Live is useful for conversation, but voice recognition can mishear names, numbers and technical terms. For exact instructions, paste the text into a normal chat and ask Gemini to review it.

What Does Not Work

Gemini Live is not a universal desktop voice feature. Google's help guidance says it is not available in the Gemini web app or Google Messages. Camera and screen sharing also depend on supported Android devices and should not be assumed to appear on every account.

Safety and Limitations

Google asks users to respect other people's privacy and get permission before recording or including them in a Live chat. Treat camera and screen sharing as a deliberate action. Do not assume that a private conversation is safe to share with a screen or camera when other people are nearby.

Avoid showing passwords, one-time codes, payment information, health records or confidential company data. If you use connected apps, check which services are connected and what actions Gemini can take. Work or school accounts may have administrator controls and different connected-app support.

Gemini can misunderstand a scene or give an incorrect answer. For safety-critical questions, verify the information independently. Do not follow instructions that could damage a device, spend money or change account settings without reviewing the action first.

Key Takeaways

  • Use the Gemini mobile app on a supported Android device to start Live.
  • Live is an ongoing voice conversation, not just dictation for one prompt.
  • Camera and screen sharing are optional and require careful privacy checks.
  • Google says Live is not currently available in the Gemini web app or Google Messages.
  • Feature access can vary by account, language, region and rollout.

FAQ

How do I talk to Gemini instead of typing?

Open the Gemini app on a supported Android device, tap Live or swipe left, then start speaking. You can also use Google's supported voice phrase when the feature is available.

Is Gemini Live the same as the microphone button?

No. Voice input can capture a spoken prompt. Live keeps a conversation open so you can speak, interrupt and continue without typing each message.

Can I use Gemini Live on a computer?

Google's current help page says Gemini Live is not available in the Gemini web app or Gemini in Google Messages. Check the current help page for future availability changes.

Can Gemini Live see my screen?

Yes, on supported Android devices you can share your screen during Live. Google says the full screen is shared, so close private material before you turn it on.

Can I change Gemini's voice?

Yes, where the setting is available. Google's help page says the voice setting applies to Gemini Live and other Gemini app features that respond verbally.

Why is the Live button missing?

Update the Google app, confirm that you are signed in, check the account's access and review language and device availability. Some features roll out gradually.
