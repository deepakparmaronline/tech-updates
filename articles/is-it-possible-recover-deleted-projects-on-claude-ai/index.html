Can You Recover Deleted Projects on Claude AI? A Guide

If a Claude project was archived, you can unarchive it. If you confirmed deletion, Anthropic's public help docs show no self-service restore option.

Key takeaways

  • Anthropic's public project guide documents deletion but does not show an undo or restore process after confirmation.
  • Check your account, workspace and shared project tabs before assuming that a project was deleted.

Short Answer

It depends on what happened to the project. If you archived it, you can bring it back. Anthropic says archived Claude projects remain accessible and can be unarchived. The project keeps its knowledge and sharing permissions while it is archived.

If you selected Delete and confirmed the action, the public Claude help guide does not show a self service restore button or an undo process. That means you should not assume a deleted project can be recovered. If the project contained important work, contact Claude Support as soon as possible, but Anthropic does not promise recovery in the public instructions.

Before treating the project as permanently gone, check that you are in the right account and workspace. A project can be hidden because it is archived, shared by another person or listed under a different tab.

What the Question Really Means

People often say a project is deleted when it has only disappeared from the main list. Claude has several places where a project can appear. The Projects page includes your own projects, projects shared with your organisation and projects shared with you. An archived project is also kept separately from the active list.

There is another difference between a project and a conversation. A project can hold instructions and knowledge that apply to its chats. A chat can also be moved into or out of a project. If you cannot see a project, the conversation may still exist elsewhere in your chat history.

The important question is whether you archived the project, lost access to a shared project, changed accounts or confirmed deletion. Each situation needs a different check. Reinstalling the app will not bring back a project that was removed from the account on Anthropic's servers.

How It Works

Anthropic's project management guide explains how projects are organised. A project can contain instructions and a knowledge base. Chats inside the project can use that project context. A project can also be shared with other members on Team or Enterprise plans, subject to the organisation's settings.

Archiving is designed for projects that you may need later but do not want in the active list. Anthropic says archiving does not reset sharing permissions or remove members. Project knowledge is preserved, and the project is restored as it was when you unarchive it. Archived projects remain accessible from the archived projects area.

Deleting is different. The help guide says you must unarchive a project before you can delete it. The user then selects Delete and confirms the action. The guide explains how to perform the deletion, but it does not list a recovery or undo step after confirmation. That is why it is important to check the archived area first.

Anthropic's privacy article about deleting data says consumer conversations are removed from the conversation history immediately after deletion and automatically deleted from the backend within 30 days, subject to stated exceptions. That article describes conversations. It should not be treated as a promise that a deleted project can be restored.

Practical Steps

1. Confirm the account and workspace

Check the email address and sign in method you use for Claude. If you have a personal account and a work account, switch to the one where the project was created. A project will not appear in the wrong account just because the display name looks similar.

If your organisation manages Claude, check the workspace selected in the account menu. A project created in a work workspace may not appear in your personal project list.

2. Check all project tabs

Open the Projects page and look through Your projects, Organization and Shared with you. If another person created the project, it may be listed under the shared area rather than your own projects.

If you no longer have permission to view a shared project, ask its owner or workspace administrator whether your access changed. Do not create a duplicate project until you know whether the original still exists.

3. Check archived projects

Look for the archived projects tab or archived area on the Projects page. Anthropic says archived projects remain accessible. If you find the missing project, open its menu and choose Unarchive. You can also open the archived project and use its menu to restore it.

After unarchiving, confirm that the project instructions, knowledge and shared members are present. The help guide says those details are preserved during archiving.

4. Search for chats outside the project

A conversation may have been moved out of a project. Search your chat history for a distinctive phrase from the conversation. If you find it, open the chat and check whether it can be moved into an active project.

This does not recreate the original project structure, but it may help you find useful work that you thought was missing. Save important content in a separate document before making more changes.

5. If you confirmed deletion, contact support

If the project is not in your own, organisation, shared or archived areas, and you remember confirming Delete, contact Claude Support. Include the project name, the account or workspace where it lived and the approximate time when you deleted it. Do not send passwords or private access tokens.

Ask whether any recovery is possible, but keep expectations realistic. Anthropic's public project guide does not promise a restore option. If the data is important for a business process, also tell your workspace administrator so they can check any internal copies or exports.

Common Problems

One common problem is checking only the main project list. Archived projects can be easy to miss because they are not shown in the same place as active work. Check the archived area before assuming the project has been deleted.

Another problem is using the wrong account. A user may have a personal Claude account and access through an organisation. Projects do not automatically appear in every workspace. Confirm the account and workspace before contacting support.

A third problem is losing access to a shared project. The project may still exist even if you can no longer see it. Ask the project creator or administrator to check the sharing settings. Anthropic's guide says shared projects can have view and edit permissions, and the creator can remove access.

You may also find a chat but not the project. Chats can be moved between projects or removed from a project. Finding the conversation does not mean the original instructions, files and project setup have been restored. Save the conversation and rebuild only the parts you need.

What Does Not Work

Creating a new project with the same name does not restore the original project. It creates a separate workspace with no automatic connection to the deleted project's knowledge or history.

Clearing browser data, reinstalling the mobile app or signing out and back in will not reverse a confirmed server side deletion. Those steps may help with a display problem, but they cannot restore data that is no longer available to the account.

Avoid websites that claim they can recover Claude projects if you provide your login details. A third party cannot safely promise access to Anthropic's account data. Never share your password, session cookie or API key with a recovery service.

Safety and Limitations

Treat the official help pages as the limit of what is publicly confirmed. Anthropic explains how to archive, unarchive and delete projects. It does not document a self service recovery flow after a project has been deleted. The privacy article explains the deletion of conversations, not a project restore service.

For future protection, keep a copy of important instructions, reference files and final outputs outside a project. Follow your organisation's data retention rules if the project contains customer, legal or confidential information. Before deleting a project, check its contents and confirm that no other team member needs it.

If you manage a work workspace, agree on an archive process before deleting completed projects. An archive keeps the project available for later reference. Deletion should be reserved for cases where the team has confirmed that the content is no longer needed.

Key Takeaways

  • Check the archived projects area first. Archived projects can be unarchived.
  • If you confirmed deletion, Anthropic's public guide does not show a restore option.
  • Check the account, workspace and shared project tabs before contacting support.

FAQ

Is it possible to recover deleted projects on Claude AI?

If the project was archived, you can unarchive it. If you confirmed deletion, Anthropic's public help guide does not document a self service restore option. Contact support if the project is important, but do not assume it can be recovered.

How do I find archived projects in Claude?

Open the Projects page and look for the archived projects area. Anthropic says archived projects remain accessible and can be opened from the archived projects tab.

Can I unarchive a Claude project?

Yes. Find the project in the archived area, open its menu and select Unarchive. The help guide says project knowledge and sharing permissions are preserved during archiving.

Does deleting a Claude project delete its chats?

Anthropic's public project guide explains the deletion steps but does not fully describe every data consequence. Check the current help page or ask Claude Support for your account and workspace.

Does Claude keep deleted conversations?

Anthropic says deleted consumer conversations are removed from the conversation history immediately and automatically deleted from its backend within 30 days, subject to stated exceptions. This does not mean a project can be restored.

Can Claude Support restore a deleted project?

The public help pages do not promise that support can restore a deleted project. Contact support promptly and provide the project details, but plan for the possibility that the content cannot be recovered.
