22:T3133,<p>TSMC&#39;s board approved up to $1 billion in combined investments across Anthropic and OpenAI, according to the company&#39;s November 10, 2026 board notice. The approval sets a ceiling of up to $500 million for Anthropic common shares at its initial public offering price and up to $500 million for OpenAI preferred stock.</p>
<p>The decision places the chipmaker in two roles at once: a major supplier to the AI industry and a potential investor in two of its most visible model developers. The official notice is dated November 10 but does not show a clock time. Reuters reported the decision at 09:15 AM Eastern Time, or about 19:45 in India.</p>
<h2 id="news-summary">News Summary</h2><p>TSMC&#39;s board approved two strategic investments on November 10, 2026. The first is an investment of no more than $500 million in Anthropic common shares at the company&#39;s IPO price. The second is an investment of no more than $500 million in OpenAI Group PBC preferred stock. The combined maximum is $1 billion.</p>
<p>The official board notice confirms the amounts and share types. Reuters independently reported the decision and described it as a sign of TSMC&#39;s confidence in continued demand for AI infrastructure. The wording matters: the board approved investments up to the stated amounts. The notice does not say that the full $1 billion had already been transferred or that all closing conditions had been completed.</p>
<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened</h2><p>TSMC published its Board of Directors Meeting Resolutions for November 10, 2026. In the fifth listed resolution, the board approved an investment in Anthropic at its per-share initial public offering price, up to $500 million, to acquire common shares. The same resolution approved an investment of up to $500 million to acquire OpenAI preferred stock.</p>
<p>This is a direct company disclosure, not only a report based on unnamed sources. The notice is published on TSMC&#39;s public relations website and identifies the board decision and the maximum amounts. Reuters reported the move on the same day, adding context about the companies and the broader AI investment market.</p>
<p>The distinction between approval and completion is important. A board resolution authorizes an investment within stated terms. It does not, by itself, establish the exact number of shares purchased, the final cash amount, the completion date, or any other terms that are not listed in the notice. The public notice does not publish a detailed shareholder agreement for either investment.</p>
<p>The Anthropic investment is tied to the company&#39;s IPO price. That means the final number of shares depends on the price at which the shares are offered and the amount TSMC ultimately invests. The OpenAI investment is described as preferred stock. The notice does not provide a full term sheet or a valuation for the preferred shares.</p>
<h2 id="why-it-matters">Why It Matters</h2><p>TSMC is a central supplier in the semiconductor supply chain used by AI companies. Advanced processors and packaging capacity are important inputs for training and serving large models. A strategic investment in AI developers gives TSMC a possible financial stake in companies that also depend on the wider chip ecosystem.</p>
<p>The move is notable because it connects two sides of the AI market. TSMC earns revenue by manufacturing chips and related components. It may also gain investment exposure to companies that use large amounts of computing capacity. This does not mean that TSMC controls those companies or that it will supply them on exclusive terms. The public board notice does not announce any preferential chip allocation.</p>
<p>For Anthropic and OpenAI, a strategic investment from a major semiconductor supplier can be one part of a broader funding picture. It may align interests around long-term infrastructure needs. But the investment amount should not be confused with a complete funding round, a supply contract, or a guarantee that future compute capacity will be available at a particular price.</p>
<p>The wider market will watch whether more infrastructure suppliers take equity positions in AI developers. If that pattern grows, investors may need to understand how suppliers manage commercial relationships, capital exposure, and potential conflicts. The public information released on November 10 is not enough to answer every governance question, but it makes the relationship more visible.</p>
<h2 id="key-details">Key Details</h2><p>The official TSMC notice lists the Anthropic investment as common shares purchased at the company&#39;s IPO price, up to $500 million. It lists the OpenAI investment as preferred stock, also up to $500 million. Those descriptions are the most reliable way to state the approved terms without adding details that have not been disclosed.</p>
<p>Reuters reported the decision at 09:15 AM ET on November 10, 2026. The syndicated Reuters copy displayed by The Economic Times carried a timestamp of 07:44:58 PM IST. TSMC&#39;s official notice is dated November 10, but the page does not show the time at which it was posted. Both the official notice date and the independent report fall inside the 24-hour research window used for this article.</p>
<p>The official board notice also lists other board decisions, including capital appropriations for capacity and research spending. Those items are separate resolutions. They should not be added to the Anthropic and OpenAI investment figure. The $1 billion figure refers only to the two approved strategic investments described in the fifth resolution.</p>
<p>The public information does not include an exact closing date, the final share count, the percentage ownership TSMC may receive, or whether the full approved amounts will be used. Until a further filing or company statement provides those details, the careful wording is &quot;approved up to&quot; rather than &quot;completed a $1 billion investment.&quot;</p>
<h2 id="who-is-affected">Who Is Affected</h2><p>Anthropic and OpenAI are directly named in the board resolution. Both companies are major participants in the AI model market and rely on large-scale computing infrastructure. The investment could give TSMC a financial interest in their growth, but the notice does not describe any change to their product roadmaps or chip procurement contracts.</p>
<p>TSMC shareholders and investors will want to understand the expected return, timing, and risk of the investments. Equity in private or newly public AI companies can carry different risks from the manufacturing business. The final economics depend on the amount invested, share terms, market valuation, and the performance of the companies.</p>
<p>Other chip suppliers and cloud providers may also watch the development. The investment illustrates how AI infrastructure relationships can extend beyond buying and selling components. It does not prove that every supplier will adopt the same approach. Each company has its own capital priorities, customer relationships, and risk limits.</p>
<p>Customers of AI services should not expect an immediate change in model access, pricing, or performance because of this board approval. Those product decisions are made through separate commercial and operational arrangements. The investment is a corporate finance development, not a consumer feature release.</p>
<h2 id="what-happens-next">What Happens Next</h2><p>The next confirmation would be a later company filing or announcement that provides more detail about the closing of the investments. For Anthropic, the relevant detail is the IPO price and the number of shares acquired. For OpenAI, investors may look for disclosures about the preferred-stock issuance and the terms attached to it.</p>
<p>TSMC may also clarify the timing and final amount if the transactions proceed. The board notice is sufficient to confirm approval, but it does not provide a full investment agreement. Do not assume that the full maximum will be used until the company reports that it has completed the transactions.</p>
<p>The market will also watch how TSMC describes AI demand and its capital plan in future financial updates. The investment decision is one signal of strategic interest. It is not a standalone forecast of chip revenue or a guarantee that AI spending will continue at the same rate.</p>
<p>For developers and AI businesses, the practical point is that semiconductor capacity remains central to the model market. Investment from a chipmaker can align long-term interests, but product availability still depends on manufacturing capacity, packaging, power, data centres, and commercial agreements. The board resolution does not remove those constraints.</p>
<h2 id="what-we-know-and-do-not-know">What We Know and Do Not Know</h2><p>We know that TSMC issued a board-resolution notice dated November 10, 2026. We know the board approved up to $500 million for Anthropic common shares at IPO price and up to $500 million for OpenAI preferred stock. Reuters reported the decision on the same day and gave additional market context.</p>
<p>We do not know from the public notice whether the full amounts have been invested, when the transactions will close, how many shares TSMC will receive, or what percentage of either company it may own. The notice does not announce an exclusive chip supply agreement, a priority allocation, or a change in the companies&#39; product plans.</p>
<p>The distinction between a board-approved ceiling and a completed transaction should remain clear in any summary. The combined amount can be described as up to $1 billion. It should not be described as a $1 billion cash transfer already completed. Further company disclosures may add details, but they should be checked before the story is updated.</p>
