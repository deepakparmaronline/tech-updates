24:T335b,<h2 id="short-answer">Short Answer</h2><p>Yes, ChatGPT processes the information you enter, and OpenAI may share some data with service providers or other parties for purposes described in its privacy policy. That does not mean every conversation is automatically used to train models. Training use depends on the account type, settings, and the way you interact with the service.</p>
<p>For a personal account, you can turn off the setting called Improve the model for everyone. OpenAI says new conversations will then not be used to improve its models. Temporary Chat adds different protections: it stays out of your history, does not create memories, and is not used to improve models while it remains temporary. OpenAI may still keep a copy for safety for up to 30 days.</p>
<p>The useful answer is not simply yes or no. Check which account you use, what settings are active, and what type of information you are about to enter.</p>
<p>Official references: <a href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7730893-data-controls-faq">OpenAI Data Controls</a>, <a href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/5722486-how-your-data-is-used-to-improve-model-performance">How OpenAI uses data to improve models</a>, and the <a href="https://openai.com/policies/privacy-policy/">OpenAI Privacy Policy</a>.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-question-really-means">What the Question Really Means</h2><p>People use the phrase &quot;share your data&quot; to mean several different things. They may be asking whether OpenAI uses their chats to train models, whether staff or service providers can process information to run the service, whether a conversation can be reviewed for safety, or whether another person can see a shared chat link. These are separate questions and have different controls.</p>
<p>Model training is about whether eligible conversations help improve future models. Service delivery is about processing information to make ChatGPT work, such as storing a conversation, handling a file, or detecting abuse. Shared links are different again because they can make a conversation available to people who have the link. A user who turns off model training has not automatically deleted old chats or removed a shared link.</p>
<p>Your account also matters. A personal Free, Plus, or Pro account has different default data rules from ChatGPT Business, Enterprise, Edu, or an API account. OpenAI says business and managed workspace content is not used to train models by default. However, an employer may have its own workspace rules, retention settings, or approved use policy.</p>
<p>Before deciding whether a setting is enough, identify the exact risk you want to reduce. If you do not want new personal chats used for model improvement, change the training setting. If you do not want a conversation saved in history, use Temporary Chat or delete the chat as appropriate. If you are handling regulated or confidential information, check your organization&#39;s rules first.</p>
<h2 id="how-it-works">How It Works</h2><p>ChatGPT needs to process prompts and files to return an answer. The service may also process information to keep accounts secure, prevent abuse, provide support, and meet legal obligations. OpenAI&#39;s privacy policy describes how it collects, uses, and shares personal information. Read the policy for the full legal details rather than treating a single settings switch as a promise that no data is processed.</p>
<p>For personal accounts, the model improvement setting controls whether new conversations may be used to improve OpenAI models. When the setting is off, new conversations remain in chat history unless you separately delete them. This distinction matters because data retention and model training are not the same thing.</p>
<p>Temporary Chat is designed for a different use case. OpenAI says these conversations do not appear in chat history, do not create or update memories, and are not used to improve models while they remain temporary. A copy may be retained for up to 30 days for safety. If you save a Temporary Chat, it becomes a regular conversation and follows your account settings.</p>
<p>Memory is another separate feature. It helps ChatGPT personalize future replies. Turning memory off does not automatically turn off model training, and turning training off does not erase saved memories. Review both areas if your concern is personalization as well as training.</p>
<p>Feedback also has a special rule. OpenAI says that if you choose to rate a response or submit feedback, the conversation associated with that feedback may be used to improve models even if you opted out. If you want to avoid that exception, do not submit feedback on a conversation that contains information you do not want used for this purpose.</p>
<h2 id="practical-steps">Practical Steps</h2><p>Use this checklist before sharing sensitive information.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Check the account type.</strong> Confirm whether you are signed in to a personal account, a company workspace, or an API based service. Do not assume their rules are identical.</li>
<li><strong>Review model training.</strong> In ChatGPT, open your profile menu, select Settings, then Data controls. Turn off Improve the model for everyone if you do not want new personal conversations used for model improvement. The exact screen can vary by device.</li>
<li><strong>Use Temporary Chat when appropriate.</strong> Start a Temporary Chat for a conversation that you do not want in normal history. Remember that temporary does not mean zero retention.</li>
<li><strong>Review Memory separately.</strong> Open Settings and Personalization to check saved memories and related controls. Delete individual memories or chats when that is your goal.</li>
<li><strong>Manage shared links.</strong> If you have created a shared conversation link, review and remove it when it is no longer needed. Deleting the original conversation and managing the shared link are related but separate actions.</li>
<li><strong>Keep secrets out of prompts.</strong> Do not paste passwords, API keys, full payment details, private customer records, or confidential source code into a personal AI account.</li>
<li><strong>Check workplace policy.</strong> Ask which approved account and data classes your employer allows. A company plan is not permission to upload every file.</li>
<li><strong>Recheck after product changes.</strong> Settings names and features can change. Use OpenAI&#39;s current help page when a screen does not match these steps.</li>
</ol>
<p>The goal is to match the control to the risk. A training opt out helps with model improvement. Temporary Chat helps with history and memory behavior. Neither replaces safe handling of confidential data.</p>
<h2 id="common-problems">Common Problems</h2><p>One common mistake is assuming that turning off training deletes old chats. It does not. OpenAI says saved chats remain in history. If your goal is to remove a conversation, use the delete control for that chat and review the account deletion and retention guidance where relevant.</p>
<p>Another mistake is treating Temporary Chat as a vault. Temporary conversations can still be retained for safety for up to 30 days, according to OpenAI. Do not use it to store passwords or information that should never leave your own systems.</p>
<p>People also confuse Memory with training. Memory helps personalize responses; training controls whether eligible content may help improve models. If you want less personalization, review Memory. If you want to opt out of model improvement, review Data controls. They solve different problems.</p>
<p>A fourth problem occurs in shared or managed accounts. An employer may set workspace policies that limit which controls users can change. A setting available in a personal account may not appear in a managed workspace. Check the workspace administrator guidance rather than assuming something is broken.</p>
<p>Finally, users sometimes believe that an answer is private because it was not published publicly. Private does not mean that the service did not process the prompt. Always check the data policy and account settings before sharing information.</p>
<h2 id="what-does-not-work">What Does Not Work</h2><p>There is no single switch that makes every form of processing stop while keeping the service fully functional. Turning off model improvement does not remove chat history, erase memory, cancel account retention rules, or undo a shared link. Temporary Chat does not promise instant deletion of every copy.</p>
<p>Changing the browser, using a different device, or deleting the app from your phone also does not replace account level privacy controls. If you are signed in, the training choice can apply across devices. If you use ChatGPT without signing in, the setting may be saved in that browser instead.</p>
<p>Do not rely on vague claims such as &quot;AI never stores anything&quot; or &quot;all chats are public.&quot; Both are too broad. Read the provider&#39;s current policy and match it to the exact product and account you use.</p>
<h2 id="safety-and-limitations">Safety and Limitations</h2><p>Treat ChatGPT as a service that processes data, not as a private notebook. The safest rule is to share only the information needed for the task. Replace names with placeholders, remove account numbers, and summarize a confidential document instead of pasting the whole file when possible.</p>
<p>For work, ask your security or legal team which products are approved. OpenAI says content from Business, Enterprise, Edu, and certain managed workspaces is not used for model training by default, but that does not remove your organization&#39;s own rules. Check data retention, access, connectors, and any contract terms before connecting company systems.</p>
<p>If you use an API, read the API specific terms and data controls rather than assuming the consumer ChatGPT settings apply. If you submit a support request or feedback, review the relevant notice because those interactions can follow different rules.</p>
<p>This guide reflects OpenAI&#39;s published help pages checked on October 18, 2026. Product settings and policies can change. When a decision involves sensitive data, use the current official policy and your organization&#39;s approved process instead of relying on a general article.</p>

<li>ChatGPT processes the information you enter, but processing is not the same as model training.</li>
<li>Turn off Improve the model for everyone to stop new personal conversations from being used to improve models, subject to published exceptions.</li>
<li>Temporary Chat changes history and memory behavior, but it may be retained for safety for up to 30 days.</li>
<li>Memory, chat deletion, shared links, and model training are separate controls.</li>
<li>Keep secrets and confidential data out of unapproved accounts.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2><h3 id="does-chatgpt-share-your-data-with-other-companies">Does ChatGPT share your data with other companies?</h3><p>OpenAI may share information with service providers and other parties for purposes described in its privacy policy. That is separate from whether a conversation is used to train models.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-stop-chatgpt-from-using-my-chats-to-train-models">Can I stop ChatGPT from using my chats to train models?</h3><p>Yes. On a personal account, open Settings, select Data controls, and turn off Improve the model for everyone. OpenAI says new conversations will not be used to improve its models, subject to published exceptions.</p>
<h3 id="does-turning-off-training-delete-my-chats">Does turning off training delete my chats?</h3><p>No. Saved chats remain in history until you delete them or take another account action.</p>
<h3 id="is-temporary-chat-completely-private">Is Temporary Chat completely private?</h3><p>No. It does not appear in history or create memories, and it is not used to improve models while temporary. OpenAI may retain a copy for safety for up to 30 days.</p>
<h3 id="are-chatgpt-business-and-enterprise-chats-used-for-training">Are ChatGPT Business and Enterprise chats used for training?</h3><p>OpenAI says content from these managed workspaces is not used to train its models by default. Your organization&#39;s policies and contract terms still apply.</p>
<h3 id="can-feedback-still-be-used-if-i-opt-out">Can feedback still be used if I opt out?</h3><p>OpenAI says the conversation linked to feedback may be used to improve models even if you opted out. Avoid submitting feedback on a conversation if that exception is a concern.</p>
<h3 id="should-i-upload-company-files-to-chatgpt">Should I upload company files to ChatGPT?</h3><p>Only when the account, data policy, and your organization&#39;s rules approve that use. A paid plan alone does not make every type of information suitable for upload.</p>
