Short Answer

Perplexity AI, Inc. is not identified as a publicly listed company as of November 8, 2026. That means ordinary investors cannot search for a confirmed Perplexity stock ticker and place a standard buy order on a public exchange. A private company can raise money from selected investors without offering its shares to the public market.

Reuters reported on June 8, 2026 that Perplexity's CEO had said the company planned to go public in 2028. That statement is a reported future plan, not an IPO filing, exchange approval or confirmed listing date. Plans can change. The correct answer is therefore that Perplexity is private today, and there is no confirmed IPO date in the sources checked for this article.

What the Question Really Means

The question about whether Perplexity is public can mean three different things. Is the company listed on a stock exchange? Can an ordinary investor buy its shares through a brokerage account? Has the company announced an IPO plan? These questions need separate answers because a private company may raise money, issue shares to selected investors or discuss a future IPO without becoming publicly traded.

A private funding round is not the same as an IPO. A Form D filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission is a notice of an exempt securities offering. It does not mean that the company has filed to sell shares to the public on an exchange. Likewise, a report that a founder hopes to list in a future year does not mean an IPO is scheduled.

This article refers to Perplexity AI, Inc., the company behind the search and answer product. It does not treat every investment vehicle with the word Perplexity in its name as the operating company. The exact legal issuer matters when you read a filing or receive an investment offer.

How It Works

A public company normally has shares listed on an exchange, a ticker symbol and regular disclosure obligations that investors can inspect. A private company can issue securities under exemptions and restrict who may buy or transfer them. Private shares may be held by founders, employees, venture funds or other eligible investors. They do not trade with the same access, liquidity or price discovery as listed shares.

The SEC's 2023 Form D filing for Perplexity AI, Inc. identifies the issuer and records a notice of an exempt offering. That filing is useful evidence that the company used a private securities offering route at that time. It is not proof of a future listing, and it should not be described as an IPO filing.

A private market platform may show an estimated valuation or an indication of secondary share prices. Those figures are not the same as an exchange price. They can reflect limited transactions, special rights or restrictions that do not apply to every shareholder. A private valuation is not a guaranteed value for a share you are offered.

Practical Steps

Start with Perplexity AI, Inc. Do not confuse it with a special purpose vehicle, private fund or investment syndicate that uses the Perplexity name. Search the legal issuer, state of incorporation and CIK number. The SEC filing for Perplexity AI, Inc. lists CIK 0001972171.

Next, look for a public listing. Check the exchange and brokerage information for a real ticker, listing date and issuer name. A social media post or a private market listing is not enough. If a broker shows an instrument with a similar name, confirm the legal issuer and the market where it trades.

Use the SEC's EDGAR company search and inspect the filing type. A Form D is a notice of an exempt offering. An IPO would involve a public registration process and a prospectus. Do not interpret every SEC record as a listing document.

Finally, verify IPO statements. Read the original interview or report and note the date. Reuters reported in June 2026 that the CEO had discussed a 2028 public listing plan. That does not establish that a registration statement has been filed or that a listing will happen in that year. If someone offers you shares, ask for the legal issuer, share class, seller identity, transfer documents, valuation basis, fees and restrictions.

Common Problems

A website may show a Perplexity valuation. A valuation is not a public share price. It may refer to a private funding round or a secondary market estimate. Ask how the figure was calculated, when it was updated and which share class it describes.

A broker search may return a similar name. A similar company name does not prove that the instrument represents Perplexity AI, Inc. Check the issuer's full legal name, ticker, exchange and regulatory filings. Do not buy a different security because the name looks close.

A seller may claim an IPO is guaranteed. No future listing should be treated as guaranteed. Even when a founder has discussed a target year, timing depends on business, market, legal and regulatory conditions. Ask for the official filing rather than a screenshot or forwarded message.

A Form D may appear in SEC search. Read the form type and issuer details. Form D records an exempt offering notice. It is not the same as an S-1 registration statement for a public offering. Check the filing itself instead of relying on a search result label.

What Does Not Work

There is no verified public ticker to buy simply because Perplexity has a high private valuation or attracts investment from well known funds. Private market shares can be restricted to eligible investors and may not be transferable without company approval. An online platform that advertises access does not automatically mean that the offer is genuine or suitable for you.

Do not treat the CEO's reported 2028 plan as a scheduled IPO. The company may change the timing, choose another route or remain private longer. Do not use an estimated valuation to calculate an expected profit. A private round price, a secondary transaction and a future public market price can be very different.

Do not rely on an unofficial ticker, token, derivative or cryptocurrency that uses the Perplexity name. Such an instrument may have no connection with the company. Check the legal issuer and the actual rights attached to the security before taking any financial action.

Safety and Limitations

This article is informational and not investment advice. Private securities can be illiquid, hard to value and subject to transfer restrictions. Investors may not receive the same public disclosures that are available for a listed company. A seller may also have a different share class or rights from the founders or institutional investors.

The SEC's Form D record is a primary source for the issuer and the exempt offering notice, but it is dated April 5, 2023. It does not prove the company's status on every later date by itself. The conclusion that Perplexity is not publicly traded is based on the absence of a verified public listing in the records checked and the reported future IPO plan. Recheck the SEC, exchange and company announcements before relying on the status later.

Be cautious of anyone who pressures you to pay quickly, promises guaranteed access before an IPO or refuses to share the legal documents. Verify the intermediary independently. If the offer is cross border, confirm the rules that apply in your country and whether you are eligible to buy the security.

Practical checklist

Before responding to a private share offer, ask for the exact legal issuer and the security being sold. A seller should be able to explain whether the instrument is common stock, preferred stock, an option, a fund interest or a special purpose vehicle. Each can carry different rights, fees and transfer limits. Ask how the price was set and whether the company must approve a transfer. Check whether the intermediary is permitted to offer the security in your jurisdiction. If the seller cannot provide clear documents, do not send money. A company can be valuable and still be a poor or inaccessible investment for an individual buyer. A future IPO plan does not remove those risks or guarantee a return.
