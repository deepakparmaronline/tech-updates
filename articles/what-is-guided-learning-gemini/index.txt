21:T2eff,<p>Guided Learning is a learning tool inside Gemini Apps that helps users explore a topic with explanations, questions and study support. Instead of asking for a short answer, you can ask Gemini to teach a concept step by step, give hints or create practice questions. On desktop, open Gemini, choose More tools and select Guided Learning. Google says the mobile rollout is gradual, so the option may not appear on every account yet.</p>
<h2 id="short-answer">Short Answer</h2><p>Guided Learning is a mode in Gemini Apps designed to support learning rather than simply return a final answer. You can enter a topic, ask a question, upload supporting material and work through an explanation. Google describes Gemini as a tutor that can use guided learning, visual aids and educational resources. The goal is to help a student understand why an answer works, not only copy the answer into a notebook.</p>
<p>To open it on a computer, visit <a href="https://gemini.google.com">gemini.google.com</a>, click the tools option near the prompt box, choose More tools, select Guided Learning, enter your topic and submit. Google&#39;s <a href="https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16448384?hl=en-en">Gemini learning tools help page</a> lists the steps and current availability notes.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-question-really-means">What the Question Really Means</h2><p>The phrase Guided Learning can sound like a separate course or a full learning management system. It is better understood as a way to ask Gemini for structured help with a subject. You can use it to unpack a difficult concept, ask for examples, request practice questions or study from a document. It does not replace a teacher, textbook, lesson plan or formal assessment.</p>
<p>There are also related learning features in Gemini Apps. Google documents visual aids, videos, quizzes, flashcards and OpenStax resources. These options do not all have identical access rules. Some are gradually rolling out on mobile, and Google&#39;s current help page gives specific restrictions for OpenStax resources. Check the exact feature you want before planning a class or study session around it.</p>
<p>The best use is to make learning interactive. Ask for a short explanation first, then request a simpler example, a question to test your understanding and feedback on your answer. This creates a study loop instead of collecting a long answer that you may not understand.</p>
<h2 id="how-it-works">How It Works</h2><p>Guided Learning takes the topic or question you enter and uses Gemini to provide structured explanations and learning support. You can include an image or file so the assistant has material to refer to. For example, a student can upload a worksheet and ask Gemini to explain the concept behind one question without immediately giving away the final answer. A teacher can use a similar prompt to create a practice sequence for a class.</p>
<p>Google&#39;s help page describes a guided learning flow on desktop: open Gemini, choose Add Files, open More tools, select Guided Learning, enter the topic and submit. The page also says Gemini may use images in responses and can work with YouTube videos when Gemini Apps Activity is on. That setting matters for video based study material.</p>
<p>Google&#39;s November 3, 2026 education announcement also described Guided Learning being brought into Google Classroom globally and capabilities coming to Google Search. Those are separate product surfaces. Do not assume a feature in Classroom is identical to the Guided Learning option in the regular Gemini app.</p>
<h2 id="practical-steps">Practical Steps</h2><p>Start with one learning goal rather than asking Gemini to teach an entire subject at once. A useful prompt is to ask it to explain compound interest with a simple example, then ask one question before moving to the next step. This tells Gemini to explain, check understanding and continue at a manageable pace.</p>
<p>On a computer, go to gemini.google.com. Use the tools menu near the prompt box, choose More tools and select Guided Learning. Enter the topic or paste a focused question. If the tool is not visible, try the web version and check Google&#39;s current help page because availability is rolling out gradually on mobile.</p>
<p>Use Add Files to upload a relevant document or image when your question depends on class notes, a diagram or a worksheet. Tell Gemini which section matters. Ask it to explain the concept in its own words, then compare the explanation with the uploaded material. Avoid sending unrelated files that make the task harder to focus.</p>
<p>Request hints, multiple choice questions or open ended questions. Try answering before asking Gemini to reveal the solution. If you get an answer wrong, ask it to explain the mistake and give a similar question. This helps you see whether you understood the method or only memorised one result.</p>
<h2 id="common-problems">Common Problems</h2><p>If Guided Learning is missing on mobile, Google says the feature is gradually rolling out in the Gemini mobile app. If it does not appear, try gemini.google.com in a browser and check again later. Do not assume your account is blocked simply because another user has a different menu.</p>
<p>If Gemini gives the answer too quickly, ask it to guide you with hints and one question at a time. You can explicitly say not to reveal the final answer until you have tried. A clear instruction can make the session more useful, but you should still check the response.</p>
<p>If the explanation is too advanced, tell Gemini your study level, the course topic and what you already know. Ask for a familiar example before introducing new terms. If the explanation still feels confusing, request a diagram or break the concept into smaller steps.</p>
<p>If a file is not being used correctly, check that it is readable and that you asked about the correct page or diagram. If the file is large, narrow the task to a section. Compare any extracted numbers, labels or formulas with the original document.</p>
<h2 id="what-does-not-work">What Does Not Work</h2><p>Guided Learning does not guarantee that Gemini will teach a topic correctly. An explanation can sound smooth while containing a wrong formula, missing step or outdated fact. It also cannot know your teacher&#39;s exact marking scheme unless you provide the relevant instructions. Use it as a study partner, not as the only authority.</p>
<p>Do not ask it to complete a graded assignment and then submit the result as your own work. A better approach is to ask for hints, a worked example on a different problem or feedback on your own attempt. This preserves the learning value and helps you find the step you do not understand.</p>
<p>Do not assume that every Gemini learning feature is available in every country, account type or device. Google lists separate access restrictions for OpenStax resources, including English language and United States availability, an age limit and restrictions for work or school accounts. Read the note for the exact feature you plan to use.</p>
<h2 id="safety-and-limitations">Safety and Limitations</h2><p>Google&#39;s help page says Guided Learning features are gradually rolling out in the Gemini mobile app. It also describes OpenStax resources as currently available in English and in the United States, not for users under 18 and not for people signed in with a work or school account. These conditions apply to the OpenStax feature described on that page, not necessarily every Gemini learning tool.</p>
<p>Gemini can produce inaccurate information, incomplete explanations or invented references. For a high stakes topic, use official course material, a qualified teacher or a trusted reference. Do not upload private student records or another person&#39;s personal information unless you have permission and the school or organisation allows it.</p>
<p>Parents and teachers should review account age rules, school settings and the learning purpose before asking students to use an AI tutor. A student should know when an answer comes from an AI system and should be encouraged to check it. Learning improves when the user explains the idea in their own words, not when the tool simply completes the worksheet.</p>

<h3 id="practical-checklist">Practical checklist</h3><p>A practical study session should end with the learner doing something without the assistant. Ask Gemini to give one fresh problem, close the explanation and try the problem independently. Then compare your work and ask for feedback on the exact step that went wrong. For reading based subjects, ask it to separate the main idea, supporting evidence and unanswered questions. For science, ask for a diagram and then explain the diagram back in your own words. For language learning, request a short exercise and feedback on grammar or vocabulary. Keep a note of the concepts you still find difficult and return to those with a teacher or textbook. This turns Guided Learning into a practice routine rather than a shortcut for copying answers.</p>
