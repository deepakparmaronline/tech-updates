1b:T260c,<p>SEO is still worth it in 2026 for many businesses, but it is slower and less certain than it used to be. AI summaries cut clicks when they appear, and most new pages take a long time to rank. If your buyers use search and you can wait, it can still pay off. If you need leads next month, it is a weak fit.</p>
<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened?</h2><p>A thread on r/SEO asked whether SEO has a future for someone starting in 2026. It was about four months old when we checked, with 142 votes and 589 comments. The snippet we could see said that SEO is not dead, but low-skill SEO is. We could only read the title and that snippet. We did not read the 589 comments, so we do not report what people argued.</p>
<p>The question comes up again and again. It deserves an answer based on data, not mood. Below we use research from Pew, Ahrefs and Google&#39;s own documentation. Where a number is old or comes from a vendor, we say so.</p>
<h2 id="why-people-are-talking-about-it">Why People Are Talking About It</h2><p>Two trends collide. Search is changing, and AI tools are giving direct answers. At the same time, SEO jobs and services are still sold in large numbers.</p>
<p>On the worry side, Pew Research Center studied browsing data from 900 US adults in March 2025. <a href="https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/">Pew reported</a> that users clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared and 15% when it did not. Secondary coverage of the study adds that only about 1% of visits led to a click on a link inside the AI summary. That is a verified research result, but it is from March 2025 and is US-only.</p>
<p>On the hopeful side, Google says AI Overviews have led people to visit a greater diversity of websites for complex questions. That is Google&#39;s own claim in its <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features">AI features guide</a>. We did not find independent data in this research that confirms or rejects it.</p>
<h2 id="what-users-experienced">What Users Experienced</h2><p>We could not read the thread. Based on the title and on public research, here are the main experiences people report. Treat them as reports.</p>
<ul>
<li>Clicks are down on informational searches where an AI summary answers the question. Pew&#39;s data fits this.</li>
<li>New sites wait a long time. Ahrefs studied two million pages and found that only 5.7% of newly published pages reached the top 10 within a year for at least one keyword. Its page also says the average top-10 page is over two years old, and the average first-place page is almost three years old. This study is older, and Ahrefs is a vendor, but it is the best public data we found.</li>
<li>Low-effort content fails more often. This matches Google&#39;s warning on scaled content abuse in its <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content">generative AI guidance</a>.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="why-it-happens">Why It Happens</h2><p>AI summaries answer simple questions on the results page. A person who gets the answer has no need to click. Pew found that people ended their browsing session more often after seeing a page with an AI summary. That means informational, simple, &quot;what is&quot; content is the most exposed.</p>
<p>But AI summaries do not appear on every search. Pew&#39;s data suggested that around one in five searches in March 2025 produced one. That share may be different now, so check current data before you rely on it.</p>
<p>SEO is also slow by nature. Search engines need time to find, test and trust a site. Ahrefs&#39;s data shows that most pages do not get there quickly. Competition adds to the delay.</p>
<p>Finally, the supply of low-quality content has grown. Because AI makes content cheap, standing out requires real experience, original data or a strong brand. This is an inference, but it matches Google&#39;s repeated guidance to focus on content that helps people.</p>
<h2 id="working-fixes">Working Fixes</h2><p>If you are deciding whether to invest, use these steps. They are our method.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Check if your buyers search.</strong> Look at Google Trends, Search Console and your sales calls. If buyers search for what you sell, SEO has a base.</li>
<li><strong>Check your time horizon.</strong> If you need results in under six months, add paid ads or outreach. SEO is a longer game.</li>
<li><strong>Focus on searches that need a click.</strong> Comparisons, local searches, product research, tools and expert opinions are less likely to be answered fully by a summary. This is an inference.</li>
<li><strong>Build what AI cannot copy.</strong> Add first-hand tests, original data, real photos, and named experts.</li>
<li><strong>Fix the technical base.</strong> Make sure pages are indexed and fast. Google says SEO fundamentals still apply to AI features.</li>
<li><strong>Measure business results.</strong> Track leads and sales from organic traffic, not only rankings.</li>
<li><strong>Budget honestly.</strong> Ahrefs&#39;s pricing survey of SEO providers reports that 78.2% charge monthly retainers, and that the most common hourly rate is $100 to $150. Plan for time as well as money.</li>
<li><strong>Review every quarter.</strong> Keep what works. Cut what does not.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="what-doesn-t-work">What Doesn't Work</h2><ul>
<li><strong>Mass AI content.</strong> Google warns that making many pages without value may break its scaled content abuse policy.</li>
<li><strong>Waiting for a &quot;trick.&quot;</strong> Google says there is no special optimization for AI features and that SEO fundamentals still apply.</li>
<li><strong>Judging by rankings only.</strong> A ranking that brings no clicks does not pay the bills.</li>
<li><strong>Expecting fast results.</strong> Ahrefs&#39;s data shows that most new pages do not reach the top 10 within a year.</li>
<li><strong>Ignoring other channels.</strong> Relying on one source of traffic is risky when search results change.</li>
<li><strong>Copying old playbooks.</strong> Advice from 2018 may not fit a results page full of AI summaries.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="official-response">Official Response</h2><p>Google says its AI features use the same foundations as search. Its guide says all existing SEO fundamentals remain worthwhile, and that no special optimization is needed for AI Overviews or AI Mode. It also says sites that appear in AI features are counted in the Search Console Performance report, under the Web search type.</p>
<p>Google also claims that AI Overviews help people visit a greater diversity of sites. Pew&#39;s click data suggests that clicks are lower when summaries appear. These two statements are not necessarily in conflict: Google&#39;s claim is about diversity, while Pew measured click rates. But readers should treat Google&#39;s claim as the company&#39;s own view.</p>
<p>We found no official figure from Google on total organic clicks across all sites in 2026.</p>
<h2 id="key-takeaways">Key Takeaways</h2><ul>
<li>SEO can still pay off, but it is slower and less certain.</li>
<li>Pew found clicks were 8% with an AI summary and 15% without, in March 2025 US data.</li>
<li>Ahrefs found that only 5.7% of new pages reached the top 10 within a year.</li>
<li>Informational, simple-answer content is the most exposed to AI summaries.</li>
<li>Google says SEO fundamentals still apply to AI features.</li>
<li>Choose SEO if buyers search and you can wait. Choose other channels if you need fast leads.</li>
<li>Measure leads and sales, not just rankings.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2><h3 id="is-seo-dead-in-2026">Is SEO dead in 2026?</h3><p>No. Search still drives traffic. But AI summaries reduce clicks on some searches, and most new pages rank slowly. Low-effort SEO is the weakest approach.</p>
<h3 id="do-ai-overviews-kill-clicks">Do AI Overviews kill clicks?</h3><p>Pew found clicks on traditional results were 8% of visits with an AI summary and 15% without, in March 2025 US data. The effect depends on the query type.</p>
<h3 id="how-long-does-seo-take">How long does SEO take?</h3><p>Often many months. Ahrefs found that only 5.7% of new pages reached the top 10 within a year. Results vary by site strength and competition.</p>
<h3 id="is-seo-worth-it-for-a-small-business">Is SEO worth it for a small business?</h3><p>It can be, if local or high-intent searches matter to your buyers and you can wait for results. If you need leads right now, add a faster channel.</p>
<h3 id="should-i-learn-seo-in-2026">Should I learn SEO in 2026?</h3><p>Yes, if you accept that the skill is changing. Fundamentals still matter. Technical basics, content quality and measurement carry over to AI search.</p>
<h3 id="is-geo-replacing-seo">Is GEO replacing SEO?</h3><p>Google says optimizing for its AI features is still SEO. We found no evidence in this research that a separate discipline replaces it.</p>
<h3 id="what-kind-of-content-is-safest">What kind of content is safest?</h3><p>Content that needs depth, proof or trust: tests, comparisons, local help, tools and expert views. Simple definition pages are the most exposed.</p>
