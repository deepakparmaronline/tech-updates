19:T3bfe,<h2 id="short-answer">Short Answer</h2><p>There is no reliable one-word answer to whether Claude is safer than ChatGPT. The result depends on what you mean by safety. If you mean privacy, compare how each service handles conversations, model improvement, retention and connected tools. If you mean harmful responses, compare the safeguards and evaluations for the specific models you use. If you mean security, look at account protection, permissions and how the service handles your data.</p>
<p>Anthropic and OpenAI both publish safety information and provide controls for users. Their policies are not identical, and a safety report from one company cannot be treated as a direct score against the other. For most people, the practical question is not which brand is always safer. It is which account settings, plan and workflow give you the right protection for the information you plan to share.</p>
<p>This guide compares the public controls and safety commitments described by <a href="https://privacy.anthropic.com/">Anthropic</a> and <a href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7730893-data-controls-faq">OpenAI</a>. Check the live terms before using either service for confidential work.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-question-really-means">What the Question Really Means</h2><p>The word safe covers several different risks. A chatbot can protect your conversation from model training but still give a wrong answer. It can refuse harmful requests but still be vulnerable to prompt injection when it reads an untrusted document. It can have strong account security while a user shares a sensitive file with the wrong connector.</p>
<p>It helps to separate five questions:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Privacy:</strong> What happens to prompts, uploaded files and conversation history?</li>
<li><strong>Model improvement:</strong> Can conversations be used to improve or train models?</li>
<li><strong>Output safety:</strong> How does the model respond to harmful, illegal or risky requests?</li>
<li><strong>Security:</strong> How are accounts, integrations and tool permissions protected?</li>
<li><strong>Reliability:</strong> Can the model invent facts, misunderstand context or follow malicious instructions hidden in a file?</li>
</ol>
<p>A company can perform well in one area and still leave risk in another. For example, a model that refuses more harmful prompts is not automatically better at protecting a connected inbox. A privacy setting also does not guarantee that every answer is accurate.</p>
<p>The phrase safer than ChatGPT often hides a narrower question. If you are deciding which assistant to use for work, start with the information you will share and the actions the assistant can take. If you are deciding which model gives safer answers, define the task and compare the exact model versions under the same test.</p>
<h2 id="how-it-works">How It Works</h2><h3 id="privacy-and-model-improvement">Privacy and model improvement</h3><p>Anthropic&#39;s <a href="https://privacy.anthropic.com/">Privacy Center</a> explains how consumer product data and model improvement choices work. Users should review the current privacy setting for their Claude account and understand what happens to conversations that are reported or flagged for safety review. Incognito conversations have separate handling, so do not assume every chat mode has the same data treatment.</p>
<p>OpenAI provides a Data Controls setting called Improve the model for everyone for personal ChatGPT accounts. The <a href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7730893-data-controls-faq">Data Controls FAQ</a> explains how users can change whether new conversations are used to improve models. OpenAI also says that data from ChatGPT Business, Enterprise, Edu and the API is not used for training by default.</p>
<p>Those statements do not mean the two services store data in exactly the same way. Training use, conversation history, safety review, retention and deletion are separate questions. A setting that changes model improvement does not necessarily delete an existing conversation or remove every record needed for service security.</p>
<h3 id="safety-testing-and-policy">Safety testing and policy</h3><p>Anthropic publishes its <a href="https://www.anthropic.com/responsible-scaling-policy">Responsible Scaling Policy</a>, which describes how it evaluates model capabilities and sets safety requirements as risks increase. Its safety work includes testing, risk assessment and decisions about whether a model can be deployed under the company&#39;s stated safeguards.</p>
<p>OpenAI publishes its <a href="https://openai.com/safety/preparedness/">Preparedness Framework</a> and model safety information. The framework describes how OpenAI evaluates certain severe risks and decides what safeguards are needed before deployment. Its <a href="https://model-spec.openai.com/">Model Spec</a> also describes the intended behaviour of its assistants.</p>
<p>These documents explain each company&#39;s approach. They do not create a direct, independent ranking of Claude against ChatGPT. The companies use different model families, test sets, thresholds and release processes. A result from one evaluation cannot be treated as proof that one entire product is safer.</p>
<h3 id="tools-and-connected-data">Tools and connected data</h3><p>Both assistants can work with files, search results and connected tools, depending on the product and plan. This changes the risk. A model that can read a document may encounter instructions written inside that document. A model connected to email or a cloud drive may have access to information that a normal chat does not.</p>
<p>The key control is permission. Give an assistant only the access needed for the task. Review which apps are connected, what they can read and whether they can take actions. Ask for confirmation before sending messages, changing records or sharing files.</p>
<h2 id="practical-steps">Practical Steps</h2><h3 id="1-check-the-data-setting-before-sharing-information">1. Check the data setting before sharing information</h3><p>In ChatGPT, open Settings and Data Controls. Review the Improve the model for everyone choice and turn it off if you do not want new personal conversations used for model improvement. Temporary Chat is a separate option for conversations that should not appear in normal history.</p>
<p>In Claude, open the privacy settings and review the model improvement choice described by Anthropic. Check whether the conversation is in a normal or incognito mode and read the current privacy explanation for that mode. Do not rely on an old screenshot because settings can change.</p>
<h3 id="2-use-a-business-plan-for-business-information">2. Use a business plan for business information</h3><p>If your company handles customer records, source code, contracts or employee information, use an account that your organisation has approved. Business and enterprise plans can offer contractual data controls, administration and security features that are not available in the same way on a personal account.</p>
<p>Ask your security or legal team to review the actual terms. Confirm data retention, regional processing, access controls, audit logs, connector permissions and whether data is used for model improvement. A business label alone is not enough. The contract and settings matter.</p>
<h3 id="3-remove-secrets-before-uploading-files">3. Remove secrets before uploading files</h3><p>Do not paste passwords, API keys, private tokens, customer identifiers or production credentials into a chat. Replace sensitive values with placeholders before asking for help. If you need code review, remove secrets and personal data from logs first.</p>
<p>This is useful regardless of which assistant you choose. A model may not need the real customer name or live API key to explain a query, debug a function or summarise a document. Share the smallest amount of information that solves the task.</p>
<h3 id="4-review-connected-apps-and-tool-permissions">4. Review connected apps and tool permissions</h3><p>Before connecting email, storage, calendars or code repositories, check the permission screen. Remove integrations you no longer use. Use read-only access where possible and require confirmation for actions that affect other people or business records.</p>
<p>A prompt can also contain instructions that are not trustworthy. If the assistant reads a web page, PDF or email, treat the content as data rather than as a command to change account settings or reveal secrets. Keep the user in control of important actions.</p>
<h3 id="5-verify-important-answers">5. Verify important answers</h3><p>For medical, legal, financial, security and compliance work, use the assistant to organise questions or explain a source. Do not treat its answer as final approval. Open the cited document, check the date and confirm that the source supports the claim.</p>
<p>The same rule applies to code. Run tests, inspect generated commands and review changes before deploying them. A confident explanation from Claude or ChatGPT is not a substitute for a reproducible check.</p>
<h2 id="common-problems">Common Problems</h2><p><strong>Turning off training is not the same as deleting history.</strong> A data improvement setting controls one use of data. Conversation history, retention and deletion are separate controls. Read the service&#39;s current privacy explanation before assuming a chat has disappeared.</p>
<p><strong>A temporary or incognito chat is not a promise of zero processing.</strong> These modes can change how a conversation is used or displayed, but the provider may still process information for service delivery, security or abuse prevention under its terms.</p>
<p><strong>A safety policy is not a guarantee.</strong> Models can still produce wrong answers, miss context or follow unsafe instructions. Use verification for important decisions.</p>
<p><strong>A business plan does not remove every risk.</strong> It can add administrative and contractual controls, but employees can still share too much data, grant excessive access or approve a harmful action.</p>
<p><strong>A benchmark may not match your task.</strong> A test of harmful text responses does not measure privacy, connector security or factual accuracy. Compare the risk that matters to your workflow.</p>
<h2 id="what-does-not-work">What Does Not Work</h2><p>Do not decide that Claude is safer because one screenshot shows a refusal that ChatGPT did not give. A single prompt is not a controlled evaluation. The answer can change with the model version, system instructions, account settings and conversation context.</p>
<p>Do not assume that ChatGPT is unsafe because a personal account has a model improvement setting. OpenAI provides controls, and business, enterprise, education and API data have different default terms. Check the account type before making a claim.</p>
<p>Do not assume that Claude is safer because Anthropic publishes a responsible scaling policy. The policy is useful evidence of the company&#39;s approach, but it does not guarantee that every response is correct or that every user workflow is secure.</p>
<p>Do not use a chatbot as the only control for confidential data. Access management, encryption, retention, review and staff training still matter.</p>
<h2 id="safety-and-limitations">Safety and Limitations</h2><p>Public safety reports are useful, but they are not a full independent audit of every feature. Companies choose which tests to publish, and a model can change between releases. The results may not cover every language, task or connected tool.</p>
<p>There is also no single public score that fairly compares Claude and ChatGPT across privacy, security, harmful output and reliability. A benchmark may compare a specific model on a specific test, but that does not establish which product is safer for your company.</p>
<p>For a personal user, the most useful steps are simple: review privacy controls, avoid sharing secrets, use temporary or incognito modes when appropriate and verify important answers. For an organisation, add approved business accounts, identity controls, data loss prevention, connector review and a human approval process.</p>
<p>The safest choice is the service whose controls match your risk and whose settings your team can understand and maintain. Revisit that decision when the model, plan or connected tools change.</p>
<h2 id="key-takeaways">Key Takeaways</h2><ul>
<li>Safety includes privacy, output behaviour, security and reliability.</li>
<li>Claude and ChatGPT publish different safety approaches, not a shared score.</li>
<li>Review the model improvement and retention settings for the exact account.</li>
<li>Use an approved business plan for confidential work.</li>
<li>Limit connected app permissions and remove secrets before uploading files.</li>
<li>Verify high-impact answers and review actions before approval.</li>
<li>Do not treat a single test or brand reputation as proof of safety.</li>
</ul>
