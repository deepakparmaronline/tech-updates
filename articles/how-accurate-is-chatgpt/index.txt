1b:T2bea,<h2 id="short-answer">Short Answer</h2><p>ChatGPT can be accurate and helpful, but it is not consistently correct across every topic or task. It can explain concepts, organize information, draft text, and help analyze a problem. It can also produce false facts, outdated details, or sources that do not exist. A confident tone is not evidence.</p>
<p>OpenAI&#39;s Help Center article <a href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8313428-does-chatgpt-tell-the-truth">Does ChatGPT tell the truth?</a> warns that ChatGPT can give incorrect or misleading answers. OpenAI&#39;s research article <a href="https://openai.com/index/why-language-models-hallucinate/">Why language models hallucinate</a> explains why plausible sounding errors remain a challenge. The practical rule is to use ChatGPT as an assistant, then verify anything that matters.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-question-really-means">What the Question Really Means</h2><p>There is no single percentage that answers “How accurate is ChatGPT?” Accuracy for a spelling correction task is different from accuracy for a legal interpretation, a current product price, a medical question, a mathematical proof, or a long research summary.</p>
<p>A useful evaluation needs to specify at least four things:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The task:</strong> What exactly should the answer get right?</li>
<li><strong>The evidence:</strong> What reliable information is available to the model?</li>
<li><strong>The evaluation:</strong> How are correct, incomplete, and misleading answers scored?</li>
<li><strong>The cost of error:</strong> What happens if a user relies on a wrong answer?</li>
</ul>
<p>A model may perform well on a structured benchmark and still make a mistake in your particular situation. Likewise, one wrong answer does not prove that the tool is useless. Judge the tool against the task you need done and the evidence you can check.</p>
<h2 id="how-it-works">How It Works</h2><p>ChatGPT generates responses based on patterns learned during training and the context provided in a conversation. Depending on the model and enabled tools, it may also search the web, analyze files, run code, or use other tools. These capabilities can improve usefulness, but they do not turn every answer into a verified fact.</p>
<p>OpenAI&#39;s explanation of hallucinations describes how a model can generate a plausible statement that is not true. A model may fill a gap with a likely sounding answer rather than clearly signaling that the information is missing. It may also misunderstand an ambiguous question, use an old fact, misread a source, or combine details that do not belong together.</p>
<p>This is why confidence and correctness are different. An answer can be well written, detailed, and wrong. A short answer can also be wrong. You need to check the claims, not the style.</p>
<h2 id="practical-steps">Practical Steps</h2><h3 id="1-ask-a-specific-question">1. Ask a specific question</h3><p>Broad prompts make it harder to tell what would count as a correct answer. Instead of asking “Tell me about tax rules,” identify the country, tax year, type of income, and exact issue. For software, include the language version, error message, and relevant code.</p>
<p>Specific context helps narrow the answer. It does not remove the need for verification, especially when the question depends on current facts.</p>
<h3 id="2-ask-for-uncertainty-and-missing-information">2. Ask for uncertainty and missing information</h3><p>You can ask ChatGPT to separate facts from assumptions, identify what it cannot confirm, and list the information that would change the answer. This can make the response easier to evaluate.</p>
<p>However, a prompt such as “Do not make any mistakes” cannot guarantee that no mistakes will occur. A model can still be wrong about its own uncertainty. Treat this step as a way to expose gaps, not as a certificate of accuracy.</p>
<h3 id="3-open-the-sources-not-just-the-citations">3. Open the sources, not just the citations</h3><p>When ChatGPT provides sources, click through and confirm that the page exists and supports the claim. Check the title, publication date, author or organization, and the exact passage being cited. A real source may not support the conclusion drawn from it.</p>
<p>Prefer original sources when possible: official documentation for software, the relevant law or agency for regulations, a study&#39;s paper for scientific findings, and the product&#39;s current page for features and prices. If a claim matters, compare more than one independent source.</p>
<h3 id="4-use-web-search-for-current-information">4. Use web search for current information</h3><p>Without a current source, a response may rely on information learned earlier or the context you provide. Search can help with current announcements, release notes, opening hours, and product details by surfacing recent pages.</p>
<p>But a searched answer can still summarize a source incorrectly, miss a key qualification, or cite a page that later changed. Open the original page and confirm the relevant detail yourself. For fast changing subjects, record the date you checked.</p>
<h3 id="5-test-code-and-calculations-independently">5. Test code and calculations independently</h3><p>For code, run tests in a safe environment and inspect edge cases. A code sample may compile while still containing a security problem, an incorrect assumption, or a bug that appears only with unusual input.</p>
<p>For calculations, check the inputs, units, formula, and result. A calculator or short script can verify arithmetic, but the formula itself still needs to be appropriate. For spreadsheet formulas, test blank cells, unexpected values, and boundary cases.</p>
<h3 id="6-ask-for-a-checkable-structure">6. Ask for a checkable structure</h3><p>For research tasks, request a table with each claim, the source, the date, and what remains uncertain. For a comparison, ask which features are confirmed by official documentation and which are estimates or opinions. For a technical answer, ask for assumptions and a reproducible test.</p>
<p>Structure does not guarantee correctness, but it makes unsupported claims easier to spot. It also reduces the chance that a broad conclusion hides a missing detail.</p>
<h3 id="7-match-the-level-of-checking-to-the-risk">7. Match the level of checking to the risk</h3><p>A casual brainstorming list needs less verification than a medical decision, a contract, a financial filing, or a production database migration. The higher the cost of a mistake, the more important it is to use qualified human judgment and authoritative evidence.</p>
<p>For high impact questions, ChatGPT can help you understand terminology, prepare questions, or organize documents. It should not be the only basis for a consequential decision.</p>
<h2 id="common-problems">Common Problems</h2><h3 id="the-answer-sounds-certain-but-is-wrong">The answer sounds certain but is wrong</h3><p>Fluent writing can make a guess appear authoritative. Ask what evidence supports each material claim and verify the answer outside the conversation.</p>
<h3 id="a-citation-exists-but-does-not-support-the-claim">A citation exists but does not support the claim</h3><p>The cited page may be real while the summary misstates it. Read the relevant section directly and compare the source&#39;s exact wording, date, and scope with the answer.</p>
<h3 id="the-answer-is-outdated">The answer is outdated</h3><p>Product features, prices, laws, policies, and software commands change. Use current official sources and check the date. A previously correct answer may no longer be correct today.</p>
<h3 id="the-answer-omits-a-condition">The answer omits a condition</h3><p>An answer can be broadly right but misleading because it leaves out an exception, version requirement, jurisdiction, or eligibility rule. Ask for limitations and test whether the statement applies to your specific case.</p>
<h2 id="what-does-not-work">What Does Not Work</h2><p>Do not use confidence, length, polished writing, or a high benchmark score as a guarantee that a particular answer is correct. Do not assume that every citation is valid or that web search verifies every sentence. Do not ask the model to invent a source if it cannot find one.</p>
<p>Repeatedly asking the same question in different wording can produce different answers without revealing which one is true. A better method is to identify the disputed claim and check it against reliable evidence.</p>
<h2 id="safety-and-limitations">Safety and Limitations</h2><p>ChatGPT may be useful for education, research, writing, and analysis, but mistakes matter more in high impact situations. Do not rely on it alone for diagnosis, treatment, legal rights, investment decisions, or safety critical instructions. Use qualified professionals and the primary sources relevant to the decision.</p>
<p>Protect private information when uploading documents. Consider whether a file contains personal, financial, health, or company confidential data, and use only the service and account settings approved for that information.</p>
<h2 id="key-takeaways">Key Takeaways</h2><ul>
<li>ChatGPT can be useful without being right every time.</li>
<li>There is no universal accuracy percentage for all tasks.</li>
<li>Search and citations improve checkability but do not guarantee correctness.</li>
<li>Open primary sources and test calculations, code, and important instructions.</li>
<li>Use more independent review when the cost of error is high.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2><h3 id="how-accurate-is-chatgpt">How accurate is ChatGPT?</h3><p>It depends on the task, model, context, available tools, and evaluation method. No single percentage describes every use.</p>
<h3 id="does-chatgpt-make-mistakes">Does ChatGPT make mistakes?</h3><p>Yes. OpenAI notes that ChatGPT can produce incorrect or misleading information, including fabricated citations.</p>
<h3 id="is-chatgpt-more-accurate-with-web-search">Is ChatGPT more accurate with web search?</h3><p>Search can surface current evidence, but the answer and citations still need checking.</p>
<h3 id="can-chatgpt-invent-sources">Can ChatGPT invent sources?</h3><p>Yes. Verify that each cited source exists and actually supports the claim.</p>
<h3 id="how-do-i-check-an-answer">How do I check an answer?</h3><p>Open primary sources, compare dates and details, and independently test calculations or code.</p>
<h3 id="can-i-trust-chatgpt-for-medical-or-legal-decisions">Can I trust ChatGPT for medical or legal decisions?</h3><p>Do not use it as the sole authority for high impact decisions. Consult qualified professionals and primary sources.</p>
<h3 id="does-asking-chatgpt-to-be-certain-improve-accuracy">Does asking ChatGPT to be certain improve accuracy?</h3><p>It may encourage a more careful format, but it cannot guarantee correctness.</p>
