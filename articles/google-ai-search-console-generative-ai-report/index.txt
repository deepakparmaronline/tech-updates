24:T26de,<p>A Reddit SEO discussion about Google AI Mode captured a worry that many publishers now share: if users get more answers inside Search, how can a site owner tell whether its pages are still being discovered?</p>
<p>Google now gives SEOs a better measurement signal. Search Console has a Generative AI performance report for visibility in generative AI features on Search and Discover. Google says the rollout reached all websites worldwide by August 31, 2026.</p>
<p>This does not create a new magic AI ranking score. It gives publishers another way to study a search journey that is changing quickly.</p>
<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened?</h2><p>Google introduced dedicated Search Generative AI performance reporting in June 2026. Google said the report would give site owners a separate view of impressions from generative AI features such as AI Overviews and AI Mode.</p>
<p>The company later said these insights had rolled out to all websites worldwide by August 31.</p>
<p>That matters because AI search has often been measured indirectly. SEOs could see normal Search Console clicks, analytics referrals and occasional examples of their pages appearing as supporting links. A dedicated report adds another layer.</p>
<p>It is still not a complete AI ranking dashboard. Google does not expose a single score that tells a publisher how likely a page is to appear in an AI answer. The data should be treated as part of normal Search analysis.</p>
<h2 id="why-people-are-talking-about-it">Why People Are Talking About It</h2><p>The biggest change is measurement.</p>
<p>SEO teams are often asked whether AI is causing a traffic decline. A fall in clicks can have many causes: seasonal demand, a ranking change, technical problems, competitors, changing queries or a different Search interface.</p>
<p>The new reporting does not remove that uncertainty, but it gives teams another signal to compare with those causes.</p>
<p>Google also says its AI Search features use core Search systems to retrieve relevant pages. Its current guidance says there are no special technical requirements for eligibility beyond being indexed and eligible for normal Search.</p>
<p>That means the answer is not to abandon technical SEO.</p>
<h2 id="what-users-experienced">What Users Experienced</h2><p>The search journey can change even when a site remains visible.</p>
<p>Some queries still lead users to detailed websites. Others can be partly answered inside an AI response, reducing the need for an immediate click. A publisher may therefore keep appearing in Search while the value of each impression changes.</p>
<p>AI Mode adds another layer. Google describes it as useful for complex questions, comparisons and follow-up exploration. It can use a query fan-out approach, where several related searches help build one response.</p>
<p>For SEOs, this makes single-keyword thinking less useful. A page can become relevant to a group of related questions instead of one exact phrase.</p>
<h2 id="why-it-happens">Why It Happens</h2><p>Generative search changes how information is retrieved and presented.</p>
<p>Traditional Search often presents a list of pages. AI features can combine information from several searches and show supporting links around a generated answer. Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode may use multiple related searches and sources to build responses.</p>
<p>That does not mean a publisher needs a separate AI-only SEO system.</p>
<p>Google&#39;s generative AI guidance says core SEO remains foundational because AI Search relies on underlying Search systems. Crawlability, indexing, relevance, page quality and useful content still matter.</p>
<p>The practical change is measurement, not a new set of secret ranking rules.</p>
<h2 id="working-fixes">Working Fixes</h2><p>Start by opening the new generative AI reporting in Search Console and establish a baseline.</p>
<p>Do not judge it from one day. Compare a useful period with normal Search data.</p>
<p>Next, group pages by intent. Separate guides, commercial comparisons, product pages, local pages and news. Ask which groups are gaining or losing visibility.</p>
<p>Then compare AI visibility with normal impressions and clicks. If visibility rises but clicks fall, investigate the query mix and whether the page is satisfying an early part of the search journey.</p>
<p>Look at conversions too. Leads, purchases, sign-ups and engaged sessions are stronger business signals than impressions alone.</p>
<p>Review content quality at the page level. A citation is not the final goal if the page does not answer the reader&#39;s question.</p>
<h2 id="read-the-report-as-a-signal-not-a-verdict">Read the Report as a Signal, Not a Verdict</h2><p>Start by recording the date range, site section and business changes that could affect the comparison. If the report shows a shift, check whether it lines up with a site release, a product change, a campaign or a known search update. An observation that happens at the same time as an AI feature change is worth investigating, but timing alone does not prove the feature caused a traffic change.</p>
<p>Compare the report with the pages and search activity that matter to the business. A site-wide total can hide one important product category gaining visibility while another loses it. Review the corresponding landing pages, relevant query groups and ordinary Search performance. Then check analytics or the site&#39;s own conversion records for visits and outcomes. Keep in mind that a person may discover a brand in one place and return later through another channel, so attribution data will not necessarily describe the entire journey.</p>
<p>Use a consistent review window rather than reacting to one unusual day. Search Console data can change as reporting is processed, and small samples can move sharply when a few impressions are added or removed. Save a baseline, note the comparison period and make one change at a time where possible. This helps the team distinguish a stable pattern from a short-lived fluctuation.</p>
<p>Finally, share the limits with stakeholders. The report can help answer whether visibility in covered generative Search features changed; it cannot explain every model decision, identify every exposure across other AI products or promise future traffic. Pair it with conversions, customer feedback and standard technical checks before changing a content strategy.</p>
<h2 id="what-doesn-t-work">What Doesn't Work</h2><p>Do not buy a tool that claims to expose Google&#39;s internal AI ranking score. Google warns that third-party tools do not have access to its internal ranking or AI systems.</p>
<p>Do not add awkward AI keywords, unnecessary FAQ blocks or repeated entities just to look optimized for AI.</p>
<p>Google says there are no special technical requirements for AI Overviews or AI Mode.</p>
<p>Do not assume that one citation means a page will appear consistently. AI responses can vary by query and supporting sources.</p>
<p>Finally, do not judge AI visibility only by clicks. The search journey is broader than one visit.</p>
<h2 id="official-response">Official Response</h2><p>Google&#39;s current Search Central documentation says AI Overviews and AI Mode are grounded in Search systems and that normal SEO fundamentals remain relevant.</p>
<p>Google&#39;s Search Console documentation also says the Generative AI performance report can help publishers understand visibility in generative AI features.</p>
<p>For publishers, that is the useful distinction: AI visibility is now easier to measure, but it is still part of a larger Search ecosystem.</p>
<h2 id="key-takeaways">Key Takeaways</h2><ul>
<li>Google now provides a dedicated view for generative AI Search visibility.</li>
<li>AI visibility should be compared with normal Search performance.</li>
<li>There are no secret technical requirements for AI Overviews or AI Mode.</li>
<li>Technical SEO and useful original content still matter.</li>
<li>Business outcomes should remain the final measurement layer.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2><p><strong>Does Search Console show AI search traffic separately?</strong></p>
<p>Google has introduced a Search Generative AI performance report for visibility in generative AI features such as AI Overviews and AI Mode.</p>
<p><strong>Does appearing in AI Overviews require special SEO?</strong></p>
<p>Google says there are no additional technical requirements beyond being eligible for normal Google Search and following its quality guidance.</p>
<p><strong>Does AI Mode replace traditional SEO?</strong></p>
<p>No. Google says its generative AI Search features are rooted in core Search ranking and quality systems.</p>
<p><strong>Can I see every AI citation in Search Console?</strong></p>
<p>The report provides visibility data, but it is not a complete log of every AI answer, citation or model decision.</p>
<p><strong>What should SEOs compare with AI visibility?</strong></p>
<p>Compare it with normal Search performance, landing pages, query groups, conversions, engagement and technical changes.</p>
<p><strong>Should I optimize only for AI search?</strong></p>
<p>No. Google recommends continuing foundational SEO and creating unique, helpful, reliable, people-first content.</p>
