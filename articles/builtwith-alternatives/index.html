Best BuiltWith Alternatives for Website Tech Research in 2026

Compare Wappalyzer, WhatRuns, SimilarTech, and free tech stack checkers as BuiltWith alternatives for site lookups, lead research, and API work before you pay.

Quick Answer

If you need a BuiltWith alternative, start with Wappalyzer for technology lookup plus lead research, WhatRuns for fast free checks in your browser, SimilarTech for company and market analysis, or OpenTechCheck for a more privacy focused local scan. The right choice depends on whether you only want to inspect one site or need a large technology database, lead lists, alerts, exports, or an API.

BuiltWith remains a useful reference point. Its official site describes technology lookup, technology adoption data, company lists, and tools for sales and engineering teams. Its pricing page says individual site lookups are free, while larger research and list workflows are paid. A replacement is most useful when it gives you the same kind of evidence at a better fit for your budget and workflow.

How We Chose These Alternatives

We looked for active products with current official pages and a clear use case related to website technology detection. We compared the jobs the vendors describe, the type of access they offer, and the difference between a quick single site check and a larger research workflow. We did not run a controlled accuracy test, so this is a practical selection guide rather than a performance ranking.

A technology profiler usually infers a site's stack from signals such as page markup, scripts, headers, known assets, and patterns associated with a product. That is useful, but it is not a full audit of a company's private infrastructure. A site can hide parts of its stack, load tools only on certain pages, or have a tag that remains after a service has been removed.

We used the official Wappalyzer pricing page, WhatRuns website, SimilarTech site, and OpenTechCheck overview to confirm the main differences. Features and prices can change, so revisit each vendor's page before making a purchase. The order below reflects use case, not an unsupported score.

Best Alternatives

1. Wappalyzer for lookup and lead research

Wappalyzer is the first tool to compare if you use BuiltWith for more than a quick curiosity check. Its technology lookup and browser extension help identify the tools detected on a site. Its paid platform adds workflows for lead lists, email verification, alerts, CRM integrations, and APIs. That makes it useful for sales teams and agencies that want to find companies using a particular platform.

Wappalyzer offers a free browser extension and a free account with limited monthly lookups. Its pricing page lists paid plans for larger research and lead generation work. At the time of research, the page showed a Plus option for individual lookups and higher priced plans for larger lists and API access. Check the current plan details before you budget because usage allowances and prices can change.

Choose Wappalyzer when you need a mix of single site checks and prospecting. It may be more than you need if you only want to know whether one small website uses WordPress. For a large lead list, check the number of users, lookup credits, list filters, export limits, and whether the API is included in the specific plan you are considering.

2. WhatRuns for quick browser checks

WhatRuns is a simple option when you want to inspect a website while browsing. Its official site describes a free browser extension that identifies technologies such as content management systems, frameworks, analytics tools, hosting clues, fonts, and some WordPress plugins. It also offers a way to follow websites and receive notices when a detected technology changes.

The main appeal is low friction. You can install the extension and check a page without first building a lead list or learning a large research platform. That is useful for a developer who wants a quick clue about a competitor's site or an SEO specialist who wants to check a likely CMS before deeper research.

The tradeoff is scope. WhatRuns is positioned around browser based discovery, not the same large database and paid prospecting workflow that BuiltWith provides. If you need bulk company lists, a repeatable export process, or an API for hundreds of domains, compare the requirements carefully before treating it as a full replacement.

3. SimilarTech for company and market analysis

SimilarTech, part of Similarweb, focuses on website technology analysis and market insight. Its official site describes tools to inspect technologies used by a company, compare competitor stacks, and find companies that use selected technologies. This can suit sales research and market analysis where the question is not just what runs on one website, but which businesses appear to use a particular technology.

It is worth checking when your team already uses Similarweb products or needs technology data as part of broader company research. The workflow may be more valuable for teams that build target account lists than for a person who only needs a browser extension.

Before choosing it, confirm the features and data access included in the plan offered to you. A company focused tool can have a different cost model from a free extension. Do not assume that every feature shown in a product overview is available in a basic account.

4. OpenTechCheck for local and privacy focused checks

OpenTechCheck presents itself as an open source browser extension for identifying website technologies. It says detection runs in the browser and does not require an account to scan a site. It also shows the signals behind a detection, including visible tags, headers, and JavaScript properties.

That makes it interesting for developers and privacy conscious users who prefer to understand why a tool reached a result. The local approach can be a useful alternative when you want to inspect a page without starting a cloud research workflow or sharing a list of target domains with a vendor.

Its tradeoff is that a local extension is not the same as a large historical technology database. It may not offer the lead discovery, bulk exports, alerts, or market share views that a sales team expects from BuiltWith. Check the current repository and documentation before relying on it for a critical workflow.

How to Choose

Start by writing down the task. If you want to check one site before contacting a prospect, a free extension may be enough. If you need a list of thousands of sites using a technology, historical trends, or a CRM connection, you need to compare database size, export rules, API access, and usage limits.

Next, test the same small set of sites in two tools. Include a simple site, a large ecommerce site, and a site whose technology stack you know. Compare what each tool detects, what it misses, how clearly it explains the result, and how much time you spend confirming the findings. This is a practical check for your workflow, not a scientific benchmark.

Then check the commercial details. Confirm whether the plan includes the number of lookups you need, how often the data is refreshed, whether exports are allowed, how many team members can use the account, and whether API requests cost extra. A low starting price can be poor value if the feature you need sits in a higher tier.

Finally, decide how the result will be used. For curiosity or early research, an approximate stack is often enough. For lead scoring, market sizing, or a migration plan, verify the important technology with another source or direct evidence from the site.

What to Avoid

Do not treat a detected technology as proof that a company currently uses it everywhere. A script may be loaded on one page but not another. A tag can remain after a service is removed. Some software runs on the server and leaves little public evidence. A technology profiler can therefore produce both false positives and missed detections.

Do not buy a large plan before checking a sample. Ask whether the provider can export the fields you need, whether the records match your target market, and how the service handles duplicates. If the product is for lead generation, also check how its contact data was sourced and whether your intended use follows local rules.

Avoid installing extensions from unofficial download sites. Use the vendor's official page or the official extension store. For a work browser, review extension permissions and your organization's policies before installing any tool that can read page content.

FAQ

What is the best alternative to BuiltWith?

Wappalyzer is a close choice if you need technology detection and lead research. WhatRuns is useful for quick free browser checks, while SimilarTech is worth reviewing for company and market analysis.

Is there a free BuiltWith alternative?

Yes. WhatRuns and OpenTechCheck provide free ways to inspect website technology signals. Wappalyzer also offers a free extension and limited free account features. The right option depends on whether you need only a single site check or a larger dataset.

Is Wappalyzer better than BuiltWith?

Neither is best for every job. Wappalyzer combines technology lookup with lead generation features, while BuiltWith offers its own database, technology trends, and list building. Compare the exact workflow and current plan limits before switching.

Can these tools identify every technology on a website?

No. They infer technologies from available signals and may miss server side software, hidden scripts, or tools that load only on certain pages. Verify important findings before making a business decision.

Which alternative is best for a quick competitor check?

WhatRuns or the free Wappalyzer extension can be enough for a quick check. If you need bulk lists, alerts, exports, or an API, compare the paid research tools instead.

Should I pay for a technology profiler?

Pay when the data, scale, or integrations save enough time to justify the cost. Test a small set of websites, confirm the output fields you need, and check the current plan before signing up.
