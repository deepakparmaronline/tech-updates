1b:T2b56,<h1 id="ai-document-extraction-a-workflow-with-human-checks">AI Document Extraction: A Workflow With Human Checks</h1><p>This is a practical workflow guide, not a report of a new product announcement. AI can help turn PDFs, forms and scanned documents into structured fields, but an extracted value should not be treated as correct just because it looks neat in a table. A safer process keeps the original document available, validates each field and sends uncertain or high impact results to a person before data enters a CRM, finance system or customer record.</p>
<h2 id="news-summary">News Summary</h2><p>No specific product release is claimed here. Live external announcements could not be independently checked during this repository only run, so this article does not name a new document AI feature or attach an announcement time to the workflow. Instead, it explains a general approach for teams that want to use document processing tools without turning extraction errors into business records.</p>
<p>The <a href="https://cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs">Google Cloud Document AI documentation</a>, <a href="https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/overview">Microsoft Azure AI Document Intelligence overview</a> and <a href="https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework">NIST AI Risk Management Framework</a> are useful official starting points for understanding document processing and risk review. These external pages were not independently reopened in this run, so check their current guidance before choosing a product or setting up a regulated workflow.</p>
<h2 id="what-happened">What Happened</h2><p>There is no new launch being reported. The recurring problem is that document extraction can look accurate while hiding errors in dates, amounts, names, addresses, line items or totals. A clean JSON response can still contain a wrong value. If that value is automatically written into a business system, the error may spread into invoices, customer communications, reporting or later automation.</p>
<p>A reliable workflow separates extraction from acceptance. The model or document processor proposes values. Validation rules check structure and consistency. A review queue handles cases that fail or have high impact. Only approved records move to the next system. This makes it easier to correct mistakes and trace each field back to the source.</p>
<h2 id="why-it-matters">Why It Matters</h2><p>Documents are not always clean digital text. PDFs may contain tables, scans, rotated pages, handwriting, stamps or mixed layouts. OCR can misread characters, and a language model can interpret a field incorrectly even when the text was recognized. An invoice total may be mistaken for a subtotal, a date may be read in the wrong format, or a company name may be confused with a contact name.</p>
<p>The risk depends on the use case. A draft summary may be acceptable with light review. A payment amount, tax field, legal identifier or customer record needs stronger checks. The workflow should match the cost of a mistake, not the convenience of automation.</p>
<p>The goal is not to remove humans from every step. It is to focus human attention where the system is uncertain or where a mistake would be costly, while allowing low risk and well tested cases to move through with clear validation.</p>
<h2 id="key-details">Key Details</h2><h3 id="define-a-narrow-schema-first">Define a narrow schema first</h3><p>Choose one document type and define the fields you need. For an invoice, that might include supplier name, invoice number, date, currency, subtotal, tax and total. For a form, it might include name, address, reference number and selected options. Write down the expected data type, required fields and validation rules before asking an AI system to extract anything.</p>
<p>Avoid requesting every possible detail from the first version. A smaller schema is easier to evaluate and less likely to collect unnecessary personal information. Include a clear representation for missing or uncertain values. Do not force the system to guess just because a field is required by the downstream database.</p>
<h3 id="keep-source-references-with-the-values">Keep source references with the values</h3><p>Every extracted value should be traceable to the original file. Where the tool supports it, retain page number, bounding box, text span or a source snippet. If it does not, keep a stable document identifier and provide a way for the reviewer to open the relevant page. This makes corrections faster and helps audit a disputed value later.</p>
<p>Do not keep only the final JSON and discard the source. The original document is the evidence needed to check the extraction. Store it under the access and retention rules that apply to the data, and avoid placing sensitive files in a shared location with broader permissions than necessary.</p>
<h3 id="validate-formats-and-relationships">Validate formats and relationships</h3><p>Use code or deterministic rules to check values that have clear constraints. Dates should parse into the expected format. Currency codes should be allowed. Totals can be compared with subtotals and tax where the document&#39;s rules make that possible. Identifiers can be checked for length and known patterns. Duplicate detection can flag records that appear to have already been processed.</p>
<p>Validation is not the same as proving the value is true. A wrong date can still be a valid date, and a wrong amount can still be a number. That is why structural checks should be combined with source review and consistency checks.</p>
<h3 id="build-a-review-queue">Build a review queue</h3><p>Send a field or document to a person when required values are missing, the processor reports low confidence, multiple interpretations are possible, validation fails or the result has a high impact. The reviewer should see the original source, extracted value, validation result and reason for the flag. A reviewer should be able to correct the field and record why it changed.</p>
<p>Avoid hiding uncertainty inside a single overall confidence score. One document may have accurate names but an uncertain total. Review at field level when the downstream risk differs by field. If a tool does not provide confidence values, create review rules from missing fields, conflicting evidence and validation failures.</p>
<h2 id="who-is-affected">Who Is Affected</h2><p>Operations teams processing invoices, applications and forms need a clear route from document receipt to approved record. Developers need a schema, validation logic, error handling and safe integration with the target system. Security teams need data access controls, retention rules and logs that do not expose unnecessary sensitive content. Business owners need a way to measure whether automation saves time without increasing error rates.</p>
<p>The same principles apply whether extraction is performed by a dedicated document AI service, OCR followed by a language model, or a general AI assistant that reads a file. The exact tool changes the setup, but the control pattern remains: preserve evidence, validate values, review uncertainty and record corrections.</p>
<h2 id="what-happens-next">What Happens Next</h2><p>Start with a small, representative sample of documents. Create a manually checked reference for the fields that matter. Run the extraction, compare every field with the reference and classify errors. Measure field level accuracy, missing values, incorrect values and the time reviewers need to correct results. Do not rely on a few impressive examples.</p>
<p>Then test failure cases. Include low quality scans, missing pages, different layouts, ambiguous dates, multiple currencies and documents that contain irrelevant instructions. If the system can call tools or write to another service, test that it cannot skip approval or change unrelated records. Use a staging environment and synthetic or approved test data first.</p>
<p>Only expand after the workflow meets a clear standard. Keep a sample of reviewed results, monitor the types of errors that occur and update the evaluation set when a new document format appears. A vendor update or prompt change can alter behavior, so rerun the tests after meaningful changes. Define who can pause the workflow and how incorrect records will be corrected.</p>
<h2 id="what-we-know-and-do-not-know">What We Know and Do Not Know</h2><p>No extraction system is perfect across every document layout, language and scan quality. A confidence score may not reflect the true probability that a field is correct. Rules can catch some mistakes but not every semantic error. Human reviewers can also miss details, especially when the interface hides the source or encourages rushed approval.</p>
<p>This guide does not benchmark a current vendor or claim hands on testing. External official references were not reopened during this repository only run. Treat the workflow as general guidance, confirm the current product documentation and test the system on your own document types before processing real data.</p>
<h2 id="key-takeaways">Key Takeaways</h2><ul>
<li>Keep extracted values tied to the original document.</li>
<li>Validate field structure and relationships before writing to another system.</li>
<li>Route uncertain or high impact values to a human reviewer and track corrections.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="related-reading">Related Reading</h2><p>For connected workflows, see <a href="/articles/ai-agent-permissions-sandbox-human-review/">AI agent safety: permissions, sandboxes and human review</a> and <a href="/articles/ai-agents-privacy/">AI agents and privacy</a>.</p>
<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2><h3 id="can-ai-extract-data-from-pdfs">Can AI extract data from PDFs?</h3><p>Yes, AI and document processing tools can extract text and fields from supported documents, but results depend on layout, scan quality, language and the workflow.</p>
<h3 id="should-extracted-data-go-straight-into-a-crm">Should extracted data go straight into a CRM?</h3><p>Not without checks. Validate required fields, formats, duplicates and source references, and review high impact values before writing to a business system.</p>
<h3 id="how-do-i-check-extraction-accuracy">How do I check extraction accuracy?</h3><p>Use a labeled sample, compare every extracted field with the original and track field level errors and review time.</p>
<h3 id="what-should-happen-when-the-ai-is-unsure">What should happen when the AI is unsure?</h3><p>Route the field or document to a human reviewer. Do not force a guessed value into the database.</p>
<h3 id="can-ai-safely-process-confidential-documents">Can AI safely process confidential documents?</h3><p>Only when the service and account are approved for that data. Follow the applicable privacy terms and organization policy.</p>
<h3 id="which-documents-are-best-for-ai-extraction">Which documents are best for AI extraction?</h3><p>Start with one document type that has a clear schema and manageable risk. Expand after measuring errors and review effort.</p>
