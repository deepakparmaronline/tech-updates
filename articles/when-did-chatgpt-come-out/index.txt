1b:T2ce6,<h2 id="short-answer">Short Answer</h2><p>ChatGPT came out on November 30, 2022. OpenAI released it to the public as a free research preview, not as the mature subscription product people know today. The <a href="https://openai.com/index/chatgpt/">original OpenAI announcement</a> described a conversational model that could answer follow up questions, acknowledge mistakes, challenge false premises, and reject some inappropriate requests.</p>
<p>OpenAI&#39;s later research timeline confirms the same date and identifies the first public version as using GPT 3.5. ChatGPT Plus arrived in February 2023, and GPT 4 became available to Plus subscribers in March 2023. That sequence matters because ChatGPT, GPT 3.5, and GPT 4 did not all launch at the same time.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-question-really-means">What the Question Really Means</h2><p>People asking when ChatGPT came out often want more than one date. They may be asking when the public could first use it, when the underlying technology began, when paid plans appeared, or when the product became capable enough for everyday work.</p>
<p>The clean answer is November 30, 2022 for the public ChatGPT launch. Earlier OpenAI models laid the groundwork, including GPT 3 and InstructGPT, but those were not the ChatGPT product. OpenAI called ChatGPT a sibling model to InstructGPT because both were trained to follow instructions, while ChatGPT added a dialogue experience designed for back and forth interaction.</p>
<p>It is also useful to separate a product from a model. ChatGPT is the application people open on the web, phone, or desktop. GPT model families provide some of the intelligence behind it. OpenAI can change the available models, tools, limits, and interface without launching a completely new product called ChatGPT.</p>
<p>This distinction explains why several dates appear in histories of generative AI. November 2022 is the ChatGPT product date. February 2023 marks the introduction of the Plus subscription. March 2023 marks GPT 4 access in ChatGPT Plus. Later dates cover new models, image generation, voice, memory, search, and other tools.</p>
<h2 id="how-it-works">How It Works</h2><p>The first ChatGPT was built for conversation. A person entered a prompt, received a response, and could continue with follow up questions while the system used the earlier messages as context. That interaction felt different from a traditional search box because the product produced a direct response and could revise it during the conversation.</p>
<p>OpenAI said the launch was designed to collect feedback about strengths and weaknesses. The original announcement also documented limitations. ChatGPT could produce answers that sounded plausible but were wrong, react differently to small wording changes, and become overly verbose. It sometimes guessed what a user meant instead of asking for clarification.</p>
<p>Those limitations remain important to understanding the launch. ChatGPT did not arrive as an infallible knowledge service. The research preview put a usable interface in front of a broad public audience so OpenAI could observe real questions and failure modes. The company asked users to provide feedback about harmful and incorrect outputs.</p>
<p>The initial training approach combined supervised examples with human feedback. OpenAI explained that human trainers created conversations, compared model responses, and helped train a reward model. The product then used safety systems and instruction following behavior to shape its answers. This process did not turn the model into a database of verified facts. It remained a generative system that predicted useful responses from patterns learned during training.</p>
<p>OpenAI&#39;s <a href="https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/a253471f-8260-40c6-a2cc-aa93fe9f142e/economic-research-chatgpt-usage-paper.pdf">ChatGPT usage research</a> describes the November 30 release as a public research preview using GPT 3.5. Its timeline then records major milestones, including Plus, GPT 4, logged out access, GPT 4o, reasoning models, image generation, and GPT 5. The current product is therefore the result of continuing changes rather than one unchanged system from 2022.</p>
<h2 id="practical-steps">Practical Steps</h2><h3 id="check-the-source-when-a-launch-date-matters">Check the source when a launch date matters</h3><p>Use OpenAI&#39;s original announcement for the exact date. It is a dated primary source and clearly states November 30, 2022. A social post, summary, or anniversary article may round the date to November 2022, but the official page supplies the day.</p>
<h3 id="separate-the-product-date-from-the-model-date">Separate the product date from the model date</h3><p>When writing a report, state which launch you mean. “ChatGPT launched on November 30, 2022” is precise. “GPT launched in 2022” is not, because the GPT research line existed earlier. Likewise, GPT 4 did not power the first public ChatGPT on launch day.</p>
<h3 id="use-a-short-milestone-timeline">Use a short milestone timeline</h3><p>For most explanations, four dates provide enough context:</p>
<ul>
<li>November 30, 2022: ChatGPT public research preview</li>
<li>February 1, 2023: ChatGPT Plus launch</li>
<li>March 14, 2023: GPT 4 added for Plus subscribers</li>
<li>April 1, 2024: logged out ChatGPT access launched</li>
</ul>
<p>For later product history, use the live <a href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes">ChatGPT release notes</a>. They show how features and plan access changed over time. Do not assume a feature visible today existed in the 2022 version.</p>
<h3 id="cite-the-date-without-overstating-the-impact">Cite the date without overstating the impact</h3><p>It is fair to say the launch brought conversational generative AI to a broad public audience. It is harder to prove that one date alone caused every later change in education, work, or software. Keep historical facts separate from interpretation.</p>
<h3 id="verify-current-features-independently">Verify current features independently</h3><p>A launch history cannot answer what ChatGPT includes now. Plans, models, regional access, usage limits, privacy controls, and tools change. Use current OpenAI plan pages and release notes when the article or decision depends on today&#39;s product.</p>
<h2 id="common-problems">Common Problems</h2><p>The first common error is confusing ChatGPT with GPT 3. GPT 3 was an earlier language model, while ChatGPT was the conversational product released in November 2022. The original ChatGPT used a model from the GPT 3.5 era according to OpenAI&#39;s published timeline.</p>
<p>Another error is giving March 2023 as the ChatGPT launch date. March 14, 2023 is associated with GPT 4 becoming available in ChatGPT Plus. The application itself had already been public for more than three months.</p>
<p>Some histories use December 2022 because that was when the product spread widely or when early updates appeared. OpenAI&#39;s release notes show a performance update on December 15, but the launch announcement is dated November 30.</p>
<p>People also assume the research preview had the same features as the current service. It did not begin with today&#39;s model choices, multimodal tools, image generation, broad file work, mature mobile apps, or paid plan structure. Comparing an old screenshot with the current product requires a date and version context.</p>
<h2 id="what-does-not-work">What Does Not Work</h2><p>Do not use the release date of a model as a substitute for the release date of ChatGPT. Product and model milestones overlap, but they answer different questions.</p>
<p>Avoid citing a search result snippet without opening the source. A snippet can truncate a sentence or mix an update date with a publication date. The original OpenAI announcement and the company&#39;s research timeline agree on November 30, 2022.</p>
<p>Do not claim that ChatGPT was the first chatbot or the first language model. Conversational systems and language models existed long before 2022. The important point is that OpenAI released this particular conversational product to the public on that date.</p>
<p>Finally, do not describe the original research preview as the same service users access now. The name persisted while the models, interface, business plans, and tools evolved.</p>
<h2 id="safety-and-limitations">Safety and Limitations</h2><p>Launch history is stable, but current product details are not. If you use this timeline in academic, legal, or business work, cite the original sources and record the date you checked any current feature or pricing claim.</p>
<p>The original announcement warned that ChatGPT could generate plausible but incorrect answers. A newer model does not remove the need to verify important claims. Use primary sources for dates, policies, prices, medical information, legal issues, and financial decisions.</p>
<p>It is also worth remembering why OpenAI used the phrase research preview. Public feedback helped reveal failures that controlled evaluations might miss. That does not mean every response or later feature was experimental in the same way. Read the current terms and product documentation for the version you use.</p>
<h2 id="key-takeaways">Key Takeaways</h2><ul>
<li>ChatGPT launched publicly on November 30, 2022.</li>
<li>OpenAI released it as a free research preview to collect feedback.</li>
<li>The first public version used GPT 3.5 era technology.</li>
<li>ChatGPT Plus launched in February 2023.</li>
<li>GPT 4 arrived in ChatGPT Plus in March 2023, after the product launch.</li>
<li>ChatGPT is a product, while GPT names identify model families.</li>
</ul>
