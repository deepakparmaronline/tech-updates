27:T3b90,<p>You can ask ChatGPT to edit a photo by uploading the image and describing the change you want. The best results come from a clear instruction that names the subject, the exact area to change and the details that should stay the same. You can also use the image editor to select an area before you send the prompt.</p>
<p>ChatGPT Images can edit existing images as well as create new ones. The current <a href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11084440-images-in-chatgpt">OpenAI Help Center guide</a> explains how to upload a photo, add comments, select an area, remove an object and resize an image. The editor is useful, but the selection is not a perfect mask. Edits may affect nearby areas, so always review the full result.</p>
<h2 id="short-answer">Short Answer</h2><p>Upload the photo, then write one direct instruction. For example: &quot;Remove the red bag from the floor. Keep the person, face, clothing, lighting and rest of the room unchanged. Fill the area with a natural continuation of the wooden floor.&quot;</p>
<p>If the change is limited to one part of the image, open the editor and use its selection or comment tool. Select the area, explain what should change and send the edit. If the whole image needs a different look, describe the overall style and the parts that must remain consistent.</p>
<p>Do not start with a long list of unrelated changes. Ask for one main edit, check the result and then make a second request if needed. This makes it easier to see which instruction worked and which one caused an unwanted change.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-question-really-means">What the Question Really Means</h2><p>Use a clear version of the photo. A small, blurry or heavily compressed image gives the model less detail to work with. If you have a larger original, upload that instead of a screenshot of the photo inside another app.</p>
<p>Think about the result before you write the prompt. Are you removing an object, changing a background, correcting a color, cleaning up a product photo or changing the image style? A prompt that asks for one clear outcome is easier to follow than &quot;make this photo better.&quot;</p>
<p>Decide what must not change. For a portrait, that might be the person&#39;s face, hair, pose and clothing. For a product photo, it might be the product shape, logo, label and camera angle. For a room photo, it might be the windows, furniture and floor plan.</p>
<p>If the photo belongs to someone else, make sure you have permission to edit and use it. Avoid uploading private documents, identity cards or sensitive images when they are not needed for the task.</p>
<h2 id="practical-steps">Practical Steps</h2><ol>
<li>Open ChatGPT on the web or in the mobile app.</li>
<li>Select the plus button in the message box and choose the option to add a photo or file.</li>
<li>Upload the image you want to edit.</li>
<li>Write the change in plain language. Mention the object and its location.</li>
<li>Add a preservation instruction, such as &quot;keep the person&#39;s face and the rest of the background unchanged.&quot;</li>
<li>Send the request and review the result at full size.</li>
<li>If the edit is close but not right, ask for one correction instead of repeating the whole prompt.</li>
</ol>
<p>You can also open Images in the sidebar and work from an image there. The current OpenAI guide says you can open an image from a chat or from Images, select a spot and add a comment that describes the change. On mobile, you can open the image, select Edit and describe the change in the conversation panel.</p>
<p>The exact controls can differ by platform and rollout. If you do not see a selection or comment tool, describe the edit in the message box. The feature may also depend on workspace permissions in Enterprise or Edu accounts.</p>
<h2 id="how-it-works">How It Works</h2><p>Use this structure:</p>
<p><strong>Action + subject + location + style + preservation rules.</strong></p>
<p>Start with the action. Say remove, replace, brighten, crop, clean up, change the background or adjust the color. Then name the subject and its location. &quot;Remove the bottle near the left edge&quot; is clearer than &quot;remove that thing.&quot;</p>
<p>Describe the style only when it matters. You might ask for natural lighting, a realistic shadow, a clean studio background or a warm film look. Avoid adding several style words that point in different directions.</p>
<p>Finish with the details that must stay unchanged. This is important when the image contains a person, a product label, a logo or a familiar place. A model may still change a small detail, but a preservation instruction makes your goal clearer.</p>
<p>A useful prompt is short enough to understand but specific enough to guide the edit. You do not need special prompt language or a fixed template. Use ordinary words and describe the result you want to see.</p>
<h2 id="photo-editing-prompts-you-can-copy">Photo Editing Prompts You Can Copy</h2><p><strong>Remove an object</strong></p>
<p>Remove the blue bottle from the table near the right side. Rebuild the table surface and background naturally. Keep the people, lighting, camera angle and all other objects unchanged.</p>
<p><strong>Change a background</strong></p>
<p>Replace the plain wall behind the product with a soft light grey studio background. Keep the product shape, label, color, shadows and camera angle the same. Do not add extra props.</p>
<p><strong>Improve lighting</strong></p>
<p>Make the photo brighter and more balanced. Reduce the strong shadow on the left side, keep natural skin tones and preserve the original facial features and clothing.</p>
<p><strong>Change one color</strong></p>
<p>Change the green cushion to a deep navy blue. Keep its fabric texture, folds, shape and position. Do not change the sofa or the rest of the room.</p>
<p><strong>Clean a product photo</strong></p>
<p>Remove dust marks and small scratches from the product surface. Keep the brand name, logo, serial label, edges and proportions exactly as they appear in the original.</p>
<p><strong>Create a professional profile photo</strong></p>
<p>Use the uploaded photo as the reference. Make the background a simple neutral office setting and improve the lighting. Keep the person&#39;s face, expression, hair, clothing and pose consistent. Do not change age or facial structure.</p>
<p>These examples are starting points. Change the object, location and style to match your own image. If the first result changes an important detail, tell ChatGPT exactly what to restore instead of asking it to redo everything.</p>
<h2 id="use-the-selection-tool-for-local-changes">Use the Selection Tool for Local Changes</h2><p>The selection tool is useful when you want to change a small part of an image. Open the image editor, choose Select or Comment, and mark the area. Then describe the change in the chat panel.</p>
<p>A selection helps the model understand where to focus, but it does not guarantee that every pixel outside the selection will remain untouched. OpenAI notes that edits can extend beyond the highlighted area. This matters when the selected object touches a face, hair, a product edge or a detailed background.</p>
<p>Make the selection slightly larger than the object if the edit needs room to blend with the surroundings. If you are removing an object, include the shadow or reflection that belongs to it. If you are changing a color, select the full surface but avoid nearby items with similar colors.</p>
<p>After the edit, zoom out and compare the full image with the original. Check the edges of the edited area, the shadows, reflections, text and background lines. A close-up may look correct while the wider image has changed in an unexpected way.</p>
<h2 id="how-to-keep-the-rest-of-the-photo-unchanged">How to Keep the Rest of the Photo Unchanged</h2><p>Use a preservation list. Mention the face, pose, clothing, product label, camera angle, light direction or background elements that matter. Do not list every tiny detail if only one area is being edited. Focus on the parts that would make the image look wrong if they changed.</p>
<p>For a portrait, you can say: &quot;Keep the person&#39;s face, expression, hair, skin tone and body position unchanged. Only remove the object on the table.&quot; For a product image, you can say: &quot;Keep the logo, text, shape and proportions unchanged. Only change the background.&quot;</p>
<p>Work in small steps. If you ask for a new background, new clothing, a different pose and a new lighting style in one prompt, the model has to change many parts at once. That can make it harder to preserve the original subject.</p>
<p>If the output changes a detail that matters, ask for a correction and identify it. &quot;Restore the original logo on the bottle&quot; is more useful than &quot;try again.&quot; Keep the original image available so you can compare versions.</p>
<h2 id="common-problems">Common Problems</h2><p><strong>The model changes the face.</strong> Ask it to keep the face and expression unchanged, and limit the edit to a different part of the image. If the face still changes, start a new edit from the original photo rather than continuing from a version that has already drifted.</p>
<p><strong>The background looks artificial.</strong> Describe the light direction, perspective and surface texture. Ask for shadows that match the original scene. Avoid asking for a very different background if the subject has complex hair or transparent edges.</p>
<p><strong>The selection changes nearby objects.</strong> Make a smaller selection, use a more specific instruction and ask for only one change. Remember that the selection is a guide rather than a precise mask.</p>
<p><strong>Text or logos become distorted.</strong> Ask ChatGPT to preserve the text and logo, but check the result carefully. If the label must be exact, use a conventional photo editor to restore it from the original image.</p>
<p><strong>The result looks too polished.</strong> Ask for natural texture, realistic lighting and small imperfections. You can also say not to smooth skin or remove natural details.</p>
<p><strong>The edit does not match the original colors.</strong> Name the color you want and compare it with the original. If the exact shade matters, provide a reference color or use a standard editing tool for the final adjustment.</p>
<h2 id="what-does-not-work">What Does Not Work</h2><p>A prompt cannot guarantee pixel-perfect preservation. The selection tool is a guide, not a mask that locks every pixel outside the selected area. A request to keep a face, logo or product label unchanged can improve the instruction, but it cannot guarantee that every small detail will remain identical.</p>
<p>ChatGPT is also not the best final tool when an exact label, serial number, legal mark or precise brand asset must remain unchanged. Use a standard editor to restore exact details from the original file when accuracy matters. Do not treat a generated edit as proof that the original scene looked that way. If the image is a record, contract, identity document or evidence, keep the untouched original as the source of truth and label any edited copy clearly.</p>
<h2 id="safety-and-limitations">Safety and Limitations</h2><p>ChatGPT Images is available on supported plans and platforms, but the number of images you can create depends on your plan. Enterprise and Edu workspace access can also depend on administrator permissions. If the editor is missing, check whether the feature is available in your account and workspace.</p>
<p>Image editing is not a pixel-perfect replacement for professional software. It can change areas outside a selection, alter small text, or introduce details that were not in the original. Treat the result as an image draft that needs review, especially for product listings, legal documents, medical images or client work.</p>
<p>OpenAI&#39;s <a href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8400551-chatgpt-image-inputs-faq">image input FAQ</a> explains how image uploads work and points users to the product&#39;s data controls. Do not upload a photo that contains information you do not want to share. Check your account&#39;s data settings before using private or client images.</p>
<p>For high-stakes work, keep the original file and use a human review before publishing. A model can create a convincing edit without preserving every factual detail. This is especially important for images that show a real product, a public place, a document or an event.</p>
<h2 id="key-takeaways">Key Takeaways</h2><ul>
<li>Upload the original image and ask for one clear change.</li>
<li>Name the object and its location, then list the details that must stay the same.</li>
<li>Use the selection tool for local edits, but review the full image.</li>
<li>Ask for a targeted correction when a result is close.</li>
<li>Keep the original photo and check sensitive details before publishing.</li>
</ul>

<h3 id="how-do-i-make-chatgpt-change-only-one-part-of-a-photo">How do I make ChatGPT change only one part of a photo?</h3><p>Open the image editor, select the area you want to change and describe the edit. Also name nearby details that must stay the same. The selection is a guide, not a perfect boundary.</p>
<h3 id="what-should-i-write-in-a-photo-editing-prompt">What should I write in a photo editing prompt?</h3><p>Name the object, the change, the style and the details to preserve. Use one main change per prompt so you can see what caused the result.</p>
<h3 id="can-chatgpt-remove-an-object-from-a-photo">Can ChatGPT remove an object from a photo?</h3><p>Yes. On supported interfaces, use the Remove tool or describe the object and its location. Review the background after the edit because nearby pixels can change.</p>
<h3 id="can-chatgpt-keep-a-person-s-face-unchanged">Can ChatGPT keep a person's face unchanged?</h3><p>You can ask it to preserve the face, expression and identity, but edits are not guaranteed to keep every detail exactly the same. Check the result before using it.</p>
<h3 id="why-does-chatgpt-change-more-than-i-asked">Why does ChatGPT change more than I asked?</h3><p>The image editor can change areas beyond a selection. Make the instruction narrower, name the details to preserve and try a smaller edit from the original photo.</p>
