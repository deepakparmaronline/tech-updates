Google Search is becoming less like a page of results and more like an interface for ongoing tasks. At I/O 2026, Google described information agents that can monitor the web and fresh data for changes, then send synthesized updates. Google also says AI Mode has passed one billion monthly users and that AI features continue to drive Search usage. For SEOs, this creates a useful distinction: optimizing for an agent is not the same as trying to manipulate an AI answer. The underlying Search systems, crawlability and useful source content still matter.

## What Happened?

Google's Search strategy now includes AI Overviews, AI Mode, information agents and agentic capabilities. Google says information agents can monitor sources such as blogs, news sites and social posts along with fresh data, then notify users when something relevant changes.

This changes the user's relationship with Search. Instead of asking one question and leaving, a person can define a continuing need. The agent then becomes a monitoring layer over the web. For publishers, being a useful source becomes important not only for a single click but for repeated retrieval when the user's task changes.

## Why People Are Talking About It

The idea of an agent watching a topic is more consequential than another interface redesign because it changes the search journey. A user researching a market, product or local service may want updates rather than a one-time answer.

SEOs therefore need to think about source quality and freshness. A page with clear facts, dates, structured information and a strong topical relationship is easier to interpret than a vague page built around keyword variations. Google's own AI Search guidance continues to say that SEO best practices remain relevant because AI experiences are grounded in Search systems.

## What Users Experienced

Users may experience fewer traditional result-page interactions for tasks that are naturally ongoing. Instead of repeatedly searching, they can receive an update when something changes. That can reduce some low-value searches while increasing demand for high-quality source material.

For publishers, traffic may become more uneven. A page can be important as a cited or retrieved source even when it does not receive a click for every interaction. This is one reason SEO reporting should include visibility, conversions and brand demand rather than relying only on raw clicks.

## Why It Happens

Agentic Search needs a reliable retrieval layer. Google cannot ask an agent to monitor the web effectively if the underlying sources are inaccessible, ambiguous or stale. That is why the shift to agents does not remove the importance of technical SEO.

The more an agent acts on information, the more important freshness and provenance become. A stale product page, unclear date or unsupported claim can create a bad downstream answer. Publishers should make important information easy to identify and keep it current.

## Working Fixes

Build pages around real entities, questions and tasks instead of isolated keywords. Use clear headings, descriptive titles, useful metadata and structured data where appropriate. Keep important facts close to the content that explains them, and show dates when freshness matters.

For SEO reporting, track which pages receive impressions for complex queries and whether they lead to useful business outcomes. Also monitor crawl and indexing health. Do not create special “agent pages” unless they solve a genuine user need. The best preparation is still a strong, accessible source.

## What Doesn't Work

Do not assume there is a secret AI Mode schema that guarantees inclusion. Google has not published a magic markup for ranking in generative results.

Do not rewrite every page simply to add more AI terminology. The same low-value content can be just as weak when it uses words such as “GEO,” “AEO” or “AI search.” Improve the underlying information instead.

## Official Response

Google's I/O Search announcement describes AI Mode, information agents and agentic Search capabilities. Google says AI Mode had surpassed one billion monthly users at that point and that information agents can monitor web sources and fresh data.

Google Search Central's documentation also says SEO remains relevant for generative AI features because they rely on core Search ranking and quality systems. Its October 1 documentation update further refreshed guidance around generative AI content.

## Key Takeaways

- Google is turning Search into a more continuous, agentic experience.

- Information agents make source freshness and clarity more important.

- Core SEO remains relevant because AI Search uses Search systems underneath.

- Do not chase a fictional AI-search markup shortcut; improve the source content.

## FAQ

**What are Google information agents?**

Google describes them as agents that can monitor web and fresh data sources for a user's ongoing information need and provide synthesized updates.

**Does AI Mode replace SEO?**

No. Google says AI Search features are grounded in core Search systems, so crawlability, relevance and quality remain important.

**Will AI agents reduce clicks?**

They can change how often users interact with traditional result pages, but the effect depends on the task. High-value sources can still be retrieved and linked.

**How should SEOs prepare?**

Focus on useful source content, clear entities, technical accessibility, freshness and measurable business outcomes.

**Is there special schema for AI Mode?**

Google has not published a universal schema that guarantees AI Mode visibility.

**Should I create separate GEO pages?**

Only when they serve a genuine user need.
