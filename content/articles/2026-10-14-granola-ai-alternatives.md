## Quick Answer

The best Granola AI alternatives to compare are **Fathom**, **Fireflies**, and **Otter**. Fathom is worth a look if you want meeting recordings, summaries, and a simple way to review calls. Fireflies is useful for teams that need searchable transcripts, meeting analysis, and integrations. Otter is a familiar option for transcription, summaries, and searchable meeting records.

Granola takes a different approach. Its official [pricing page](https://www.granola.ai/pricing) describes an AI notepad for meetings, with plans for individuals and teams. Granola is active and continues to publish product updates. The right replacement depends on whether you want a different capture method, more team controls, specific integrations, or a transcript workflow that fits your meetings better.

## Best Alternatives

### 1. Fathom: best for reviewing calls and summaries

[Fathom](https://www.fathom.ai/) is worth considering if your main goal is to record meetings, review transcripts, and get summaries without writing everything down. Its official [pricing page](https://www.fathom.ai/pricing) lists a free plan and paid options for individuals and teams. The page describes support for major meeting platforms, unlimited recording and storage on listed plans, and AI summaries with plan dependent limits.

Fathom can suit people who spend a lot of time in video calls and want to return to the details later. A searchable recording can help you confirm what was said, find a decision, or prepare a follow up. Team plans may be useful when the same call notes need to be shared with colleagues.

The main question is how Fathom fits your existing meeting setup. Check the supported meeting platforms, desktop app requirements, sharing rules, and what happens to recordings when a user leaves a team. Do not assume that every summary feature is available on the free plan. The current pricing page is the best place to check which features are included.

Choose Fathom when call review and summaries matter more than a personal notepad workflow. Before switching, export important Granola notes where possible and test the new tool with a routine meeting. Compare the generated summary with the actual conversation before relying on it for a client promise or internal decision.

### 2. Fireflies: best for searchable transcripts and team workflows

[Fireflies](https://fireflies.ai/) is built around meeting transcription, summaries, search, and analysis. Its official [pricing page](https://fireflies.ai/pricing) lists a free plan and paid tiers with different allowances and team features. Its product pages describe transcription across many languages, meeting search, an AI assistant, file uploads, and integrations with other workplace tools.

Fireflies can be useful when a team wants to find information across many calls rather than only review one note. Search and integrations can help teams connect meeting information with their sales or project workflows. Its official [security page](https://fireflies.ai/security) describes data ownership, encryption, compliance programs, and its policy on using meeting data for model training.

The tradeoff is that a team focused platform can require more setup. You may need to decide which meetings are captured, where notes are shared, which integrations are enabled, and who can access transcripts. Those decisions matter if calls include customers, employees, or confidential business plans.

Choose Fireflies if search, team collaboration, and integrations are central to your process. Before enabling it, review the privacy policy and configure access rules. Recording laws and workplace policies still apply, even if the tool provides a recording notice.

### 3. Otter: best for transcription and searchable meeting records

[Otter](https://otter.ai/) is another option for people who want meeting transcription and a searchable record of conversations. Its official [pricing page](https://otter.ai/pricing) lists different plans, including a free entry plan and paid options. The limits for transcription minutes, conversation length, imports, and other features depend on the current plan.

Otter may be a good fit if your work depends on finding exact statements from calls, reviewing a transcript, and turning a conversation into notes or action items. It can also be useful for people who want to work with meeting content across supported platforms and devices.

Check the plan limits before you move a regular meeting schedule to Otter. A free tier may be enough for occasional calls but may not cover long meetings, frequent imports, or a large archive. If you need team features, confirm how sharing and administration work on the plan you are considering.

Choose Otter when transcripts and searchable records are the priority. Test it with a short meeting and compare the transcript with the audio. If names, technical terms, or multiple speakers are important, verify those details before sending the notes to other people.

### 4. Keep Granola if its note taking style fits

An alternatives article should not assume that you need to switch. [Granola](https://www.granola.ai/) remains worth considering if you prefer an AI notepad rather than a meeting bot workflow. Its official blog has recent product updates, including mobile releases, language support, and changes to its note and chat features. Its [pricing page](https://www.granola.ai/pricing) lists plans for personal and team use.

Granola's approach may suit people who want to stay focused on a conversation while using their device to create structured notes. The official product blog also explains why Granola does not use a meeting bot. The capture method is an important difference, but it is not a reason to ignore consent or company policy. Tell participants when a conversation is being captured and follow the rules that apply to the meeting.

The free plan has limits, including limits on note history. Paid plans add features for longer history, teams, and business use. Check the current pricing page for the exact terms rather than relying on an older screenshot.

Stay with Granola if it works well and the limitations do not affect your work. Switching only makes sense if another product solves a real problem, such as a missing integration, a transcript need, or a team control you require.

## How to Choose

Start by listing what you need after a meeting. If you mainly want a concise personal summary, a notepad style tool may be enough. If you need to find a sentence from a call weeks later, search and transcript access matter more. If meeting notes must flow into a CRM or project system, review the integrations before choosing.

Next, compare the capture method. Some products use a bot that joins a meeting. Others use a desktop or device based method. Ask whether the tool records audio, stores a transcript, displays a notice, and allows participants to know that capture is happening. The method affects user experience and the privacy checks you need.

Then review retention and access. Find out how long notes remain available, whether you can delete them, how sharing works, and which administrators can see them. A free plan that hides older notes may not fit a team that needs a long record. An enterprise plan may be needed for centralized controls, but verify the actual feature list.

Finally, run a small test. Use the same meeting or a similar set of notes in two tools. Compare accuracy, speaker names, action items, export quality, and the time required to correct mistakes. Do not compare only the first summary screen. The tool must work with the meetings you actually have.

## What to Avoid

Do not choose a meeting assistant based only on a claim that it is the most accurate. Accuracy depends on audio quality, accents, technical words, speaker overlap, and the recording setup. Test the tool with your own meeting conditions and check important statements against the source.

Do not assume a free plan is unlimited in every way. It may allow many meetings while limiting history, AI summaries, imports, storage, or team features. Read the current plan details before moving a regular workflow.

Do not record a meeting without checking consent and policy. Some conversations contain confidential information or personal data. Make sure participants know what is being captured and how the notes will be used. For workplace use, follow your company's rules and any legal requirements that apply.

Do not cancel the old service before exporting important notes and confirming the new workflow. Check what data can be exported, whether timestamps or speaker labels are retained, and whether files can be imported into the replacement. Keep a backup of critical records in an approved location.
