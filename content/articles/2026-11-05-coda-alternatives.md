## Quick Answer

The best Coda alternative depends on what your team actually uses Coda for. Choose Notion if you want connected documents, a team wiki and flexible databases. Choose Airtable if your workspace is mostly structured records, forms and operational apps. Choose ClickUp if tasks and project delivery matter more than document building. Choose Confluence for company knowledge linked to Jira, or Slite for a focused knowledge base with AI search.

There is one important update before you compare tools. Coda became Superhuman Docs on July 8, 2026. The product has a new name and web address, but the company says existing documents, shared links, API connections and plans continue. The official [Superhuman Help Center announcement](https://help.superhuman.com/hc/en-us/articles/46210093285773-What-s-changing-Coda-becomes-Superhuman-Docs) explains the change. If you are only searching because you cannot find Coda, you may not need an alternative at all.

## How We Chose These Alternatives

A useful alternative should replace the part of Coda that your team depends on. A tool that writes documents well may not replace a database. A database product may not feel comfortable for long project briefs. A project manager may include documents but still be a poor fit for a company wiki.

We compared the products by their current official feature pages and help material. The main checks were document editing, structured data, automation, AI features, team access, integrations and migration effort. We also looked at whether each product has a clear use case rather than treating every all-in-one workspace as the same thing.

No tool is automatically better because it has more AI buttons. The right choice is the one that supports the work your team already does without creating a new set of manual steps. Prices and AI allowances can change, so check each vendor's current plan page before making a budget decision.

## Best Alternatives

### 1. Notion for connected documents and team knowledge

Notion is a strong choice when your Coda workspace is mainly a collection of project pages, team notes, internal guides, meeting records and databases. It combines pages with databases, properties, views and connected content. Its current [pricing and feature page](https://www.notion.com/pricing) lists databases, forms, charts, connected tools and AI capabilities across its plans.

Notion works well when people need to move between a document and related records. A product team can keep a project brief beside a database of tasks. A marketing team can store campaign plans, calendars and reusable templates in one workspace. Its search and AI features can also help people find information across connected content, depending on the plan.

The trade-off is that Notion is not a direct copy of Coda's document logic. If your Coda pages depend on advanced formulas, Packs, cross-doc tables or complex button actions, expect to redesign parts of the workflow. Test one real workspace before moving the whole team.

### 2. Airtable for structured data and operational apps

Airtable is the better fit when your Coda setup behaves like a database. It is useful for content calendars, customer records, product inventories, event planning, campaign operations and approval systems. Teams can create bases, connect tables, build views and use forms to collect information.

Airtable is also investing in AI workflows and app building. Its [official product site](https://www.airtable.com/) describes AI powered workflows and app creation, while its [AI billing guide](https://support.airtable.com/articles/3378106230-airtable-ai-billing) explains that AI features use plan based credits. The amount of usage depends on the plan and the action.

Choose Airtable when the data structure is the centre of the work. It is less natural if your team writes long documents with many tables embedded between paragraphs. Before migrating, check formulas, linked records, automations, permissions and the way users create new records.

### 3. ClickUp for project management and team delivery

ClickUp suits teams that use Coda to plan projects, assign tasks, manage goals and keep delivery notes together. Its feature set includes tasks, project views, Docs, Whiteboards, automations, dashboards and AI tools. The [ClickUp feature directory](https://clickup.com/features) shows how these parts fit into one work management product.

It can be useful when a document needs to lead directly to an assigned task, status change or project update. A team can keep a brief, a task list and progress reporting in the same workspace. ClickUp also offers AI features for summaries, meeting notes and work context.

The main trade-off is product complexity. ClickUp has many controls and views, so a team may spend time setting up the workspace before it feels simple. Start with one project and a small number of views. Do not turn on every feature during migration.

### 4. Confluence for enterprise knowledge and Jira teams

Confluence is a sensible alternative when Coda is used as a company wiki, technical documentation system or shared source of truth. It supports pages, real time editing, whiteboards, databases and structured spaces. Its [official feature page](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/features) also describes Jira connections and Rovo AI features.

This makes Confluence a good option for software teams that already use Jira. Product requirements, engineering notes, release documentation and project decisions can live beside the work being tracked. Permissions and space structure also help larger teams organise content.

Confluence is not a one for one replacement for every Coda workflow. Teams that rely on free form documents, complex tables and custom formulas may need other tools alongside it. It is strongest when documentation and project knowledge matter more than building a custom app inside a document.

### 5. Slite for a focused AI knowledge base

Slite is worth considering when your main goal is to keep team knowledge accurate and easy to search. Its current [product site](https://slite.com/) presents it as an AI knowledge base. Slite Agent can connect to work tools, answer questions from team knowledge and flag documents that may be out of date. Its [changelog](https://develop.slite.dev/changelog) records recent updates to its agent and integrations.

Slite can be a good fit for onboarding guides, policies, support playbooks, engineering notes and internal answers. Its focus is narrower than a general purpose workspace. That can be an advantage if your team wants a clear place for trusted documentation instead of another project management system.

The trade-off is that Slite is not a full replacement for a Coda database or app builder. If your workflows depend on records, complex views and custom calculations, keep a database tool in the stack.

## How to Choose

Start by listing the five Coda workflows your team uses most. Do not begin with a feature checklist. Write down the actual tasks, such as publishing a campaign, approving a request, tracking a product issue or maintaining an internal policy.

If most work happens in documents, test Notion. If people spend most of their time editing records and views, test Airtable. If the team needs tasks, owners and delivery dates, test ClickUp. If documentation is tied to Jira and engineering work, test Confluence. If the main problem is finding reliable answers across team knowledge, test Slite.

Next, check how data will move. Export a small sample of documents and tables. Compare the result with the original. Pay attention to formulas, attachments, permissions, comments, automation rules and connected services. A migration is not complete just because the text appears in the new tool.

## What to Avoid

Do not switch only because a product has a new name. Superhuman says existing Coda documents continue at the new address, and existing plans and billing remain unchanged. New customers now access Docs through the Superhuman bundle, but current users can keep their existing plan. Read the official transition guide before planning a move.

Do not assume that an export preserves every feature. A document can look correct while its formulas, buttons, Packs or automations no longer work. Do not move sensitive company data into a new tool before checking permissions, data handling, retention and admin controls. Finally, avoid migrating the entire workspace before a small pilot has passed.

## Key Takeaways

- Coda is now Superhuman Docs, so a name change alone is not a reason to migrate.
- Notion is a strong document and knowledge option, while Airtable is better for structured records.
- ClickUp, Confluence and Slite serve different needs. Choose based on the work, not the number of features.

## FAQ

### Is Coda still available?

Yes. Coda became Superhuman Docs in July 2026. Existing coda.io links redirect to the new Docs address, and the company says existing plans and billing remain unchanged.

### What is the closest alternative to Coda?

Notion is a close option for teams that use Coda for documents, wikis and databases. Airtable is closer when the workspace is mainly structured records and operational apps.

### Is Airtable better than Coda?

It depends on the job. Airtable is stronger for structured data and app workflows. Coda is more document centred, so it can be easier when tables and formulas sit inside long documents.

### Can I keep using my old Coda links?

The official transition guide says existing coda.io document links automatically redirect to docs.superhuman.com. It also says existing API endpoints continue to work.

### Which Coda alternative is best for project management?

ClickUp is a strong option when tasks, goals, dashboards and automations are central. Confluence can suit teams whose project documentation is closely linked to Jira.

### Should I migrate from Coda to Superhuman Docs?

If you already use Coda, the new name does not force a migration. Review the current product, plan and integrations first. Move only if another tool solves a real gap.
