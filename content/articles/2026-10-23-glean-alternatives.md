## Quick Answer

The right Glean alternative depends on where your company's information lives and how much control you need over search. Microsoft 365 Copilot Search is worth evaluating when most work happens in Microsoft 365. Elastic is a flexible option for teams that want to build and tune enterprise search and AI retrieval. Google Cloud's search tools can suit organizations that need a custom search experience over selected websites, documents, or structured data.

Glean positions itself as an enterprise AI platform that searches across workplace tools, uses company context, and respects source permissions. Its official [enterprise search page](https://www.glean.com/enterprise-search) describes connected workplace search and permission aware results. The alternatives below are not exact copies of Glean. They are different routes to finding and using company knowledge, and each needs a fit check.

## How We Chose These Alternatives

We selected products with official documentation and a clear enterprise search or AI retrieval use case. The comparison focuses on data sources, access controls, result quality, customization, deployment, and the work needed to keep the system current. These factors matter more than a generic feature count.

Glean is a packaged workplace search and AI experience. Some alternatives are tied closely to an existing productivity suite, while others are platforms that engineers use to build a search experience. A product that works well for a Microsoft centered company may be less useful when knowledge is spread across many unrelated tools. A flexible search engine may provide more control but also require a team to build and maintain the experience.

We do not rank the products through arbitrary scores. The best choice depends on your current systems, security needs, users, and internal skills. Before buying, test the same set of real employee questions in every shortlisted product.

## Best Alternatives

### 1. Microsoft 365 Copilot Search: for Microsoft centered work

Microsoft 365 Copilot Search is a natural option for organizations that store most of their work content in Microsoft 365. Microsoft's official [Copilot Search overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/copilot-search-overview) describes natural language search across eligible work content such as emails, files, chats, meetings, and connected sources. The search experience follows the data permissions and security settings already in the Microsoft environment.

This can reduce the need to introduce a separate workplace search interface if employees already use Microsoft 365. It may be less suitable when the business relies on many non Microsoft systems or needs one experience across a wide range of unrelated applications. Check which connectors and licenses are available to your organization before assuming that every data source is covered.

Microsoft also documents privacy and protection for Copilot Search. Its [privacy guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/microsoft-365/microsoft-365-copilot-search-privacy) explains how the service relates to existing Microsoft 365 commercial privacy and security commitments. Read the current license terms and admin requirements before a rollout.

**Best fit:** Organizations that keep most work documents, email, and collaboration in Microsoft 365.

### 2. Elastic: for custom enterprise search and AI retrieval

Elastic is worth considering when the company wants to build and tune a search system rather than adopt only a ready made workplace interface. Its official [Search and AI platform page](https://www.elastic.co/enterprise-search) describes keyword, semantic, and hybrid search, vector retrieval, data ingestion, and permission controls. Those building blocks can support internal knowledge search, customer support search, and retrieval for AI applications.

The main benefit is flexibility. A technical team can control ingestion, ranking, retrieval, filters, and the way results are passed to an AI model. Elastic can also be deployed in managed or self managed environments, which may matter for data location and infrastructure choices. The tradeoff is that the team must plan connectors, relevance tuning, monitoring, access control, and the user experience.

Elastic is not automatically a drop in Glean replacement. A company may need to build connectors, identity integration, a search interface, and the workflow that employees use to ask questions. That work can be worthwhile for teams with custom needs, but it should be included in the project estimate.

**Best fit:** Engineering teams that need custom search, retrieval augmented generation, or control over deployment and ranking.

### 3. Google Cloud search tools: for custom search over chosen data

Google Cloud provides search products for building AI enabled search and recommendation experiences. Its documentation for [Agent Search](https://docs.cloud.google.com/generative-ai-app-builder/docs) describes natural language search, recommendations, generative answers, and ways to integrate a search experience into an application. Google Cloud's [Vertex AI Search overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/learn/vertex-ai-search) also explains search and retrieval across websites, unstructured documents, and structured data.

These tools can suit a company that wants to build a search app over selected data sources or embed search into its own software. They are different from buying a finished workplace search product. You need to configure data stores, choose the right edition and features, manage access, and monitor usage and cost. Google Cloud documentation notes that some enterprise features incur additional cost, so review the current pricing and product terminology before planning a deployment.

The product names and console labels have changed over time. Follow the current Google Cloud documentation rather than assuming that an older tutorial uses the same name or interface. Confirm which product and API your team needs before starting an implementation.

**Best fit:** Teams that want to build a custom search or recommendation experience using Google Cloud services.

## How to Choose

Start by mapping where knowledge lives. List the systems employees search today: email, shared drives, chat, project management, support tickets, CRM, internal wikis, and data warehouses. Mark which sources are essential and which can wait. A product that covers only half the important sources may look impressive in a demo but fail the daily workflow.

Next, build a test set from real questions employees ask. Include simple lookups, questions that need details from several documents, and questions where a user should not see the answer because they lack permission. Ask the same questions in every product. Review whether answers cite the right sources, whether results are current, and whether the system respects the access rules from the source application.

Then estimate the operational effort. A packaged workplace search product may reduce build work, while a custom search platform may provide more flexibility. Include the cost of data connectors, identity integration, indexing, relevance tuning, admin time, user support, and ongoing evaluation. For cloud services, include usage based charges and any paid features needed for your chosen setup.

Finally, check governance. Decide who can add data sources, inspect logs, manage permissions, and respond to a suspected leak. Test how quickly access changes in a source system are reflected in search. Do not approve a system based only on a successful happy path demo.

## What to Avoid

Do not assume that an AI search product automatically understands every company system. Connector availability, indexing delays, file types, access rules, and plan limits can differ. Verify the sources that matter to your organization.

Do not treat a generated answer as correct simply because it contains a citation. Open the source and confirm that it supports the claim. Test how the system behaves when the source is outdated, incomplete, or contradictory. A good search platform should make it possible for users to inspect the evidence behind an answer.

Do not compare prices without counting implementation and maintenance. A license that appears cheaper may require more engineering hours. A flexible platform may be economical for a technical team but expensive for a company that needs a complete employee interface quickly. Ask vendors to explain the total cost for your real data sources and expected use.

## FAQ

### What are the main Glean competitors?

Microsoft 365 Copilot Search, Elastic, and Google Cloud search products are worth comparing. They serve different needs and require different levels of setup.

### Is Microsoft 365 Copilot Search a replacement for Glean?

It can suit a company that keeps most work data in Microsoft 365. Compare connectors, search scope, licenses, and cross platform requirements before deciding.

### Can Elastic replace Glean?

Elastic can power custom enterprise search and AI retrieval, but it usually requires more engineering and operations work than a packaged workplace search product.

### Is Google Cloud Vertex AI Search the same as Glean?

No. Google Cloud search tools help teams build search and recommendation experiences over selected data sources. They do not automatically replace every Glean workflow.

### Does Glean publish fixed pricing?

Check Glean's current official site and ask its sales team for a current quote. Compare licenses, connectors, setup, and support rather than looking only at the headline price.

### How do I choose an enterprise AI search tool?

Test real employee questions, verify permission filtering, measure result quality, and review administration, connectors, security, and total cost.
