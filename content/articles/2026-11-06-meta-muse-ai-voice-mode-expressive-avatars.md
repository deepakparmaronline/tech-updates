Meta is adding real-time voice conversations and expressive avatars to its Muse AI agent. The rollout is starting in the United States in the Muse app on mobile, web and Mac. Users can speak naturally, hear spoken replies and see an avatar react during the conversation.

Meta's Newsroom post is dated November 5, 2026. A first-party post from Meta AI leadership appeared at 18:33:38 UTC on November 5, which was 00:03:38 IST on November 6. The Verge reported the feature at 19:03 UTC. Both timestamps fall inside this article's research window.

## News summary

The update gives Muse a voice interface and an animated avatar. Meta says users can choose voice only, an avatar beside the conversation or a full-screen avatar. The avatar uses expressions and gestures that match the conversation. Users can also choose from hundreds of voices and interrupt the agent while it is speaking.

The rollout is starting in the United States. Meta says voice conversations with Muse are coming soon to its AI glasses and Muse Charm, but it did not give a specific date for those releases.

## What Meta announced

Meta's [official Newsroom announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/11/introducing-muse-ai-voice-expressive-avatars/) says the company is rolling out voice and expressive avatars in the Muse app. The feature is designed to make it easier to talk through an idea without writing a carefully structured prompt.

The examples in the announcement include brainstorming, planning a trip and rehearsing for an interview. A user can speak while walking or doing another task, then let Muse continue working on the request. The idea is to make the interaction feel closer to a live conversation than a text exchange.

The avatar is part of the experience. It can show expressions and gestures while it listens and speaks. Meta says users can choose how visible the avatar should be. They can keep the interaction voice-only, show the avatar next to the chat or switch to a full-screen view.

Meta also says users can choose from hundreds of voices. The company describes options that can give the assistant a different style or personality. The voice can be changed through the avatar settings, according to the announcement.

## Where the feature is available

Meta says the rollout is beginning in the United States in the Muse app on mobile, web and Mac. This is a staged rollout, so users may not see the feature at the same time.

The announcement does not say that Muse voice is available worldwide. It also does not provide a full list of supported languages, device requirements or account eligibility. Those details should be checked in the app as the rollout expands.

The Verge's [November 5 report](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1028015/meta-muse-ai-voice-mode-expressive-avatar) says the feature had rolled out to the reporter in the iOS app. The reporter described the avatar animating while it spoke and noted that the agent sometimes paused when it looked up information. That is one reporter's experience, not a performance guarantee for every user.

Meta says voice conversations with Muse are coming soon to AI glasses and Muse Charm. The announcement does not confirm a launch date for either device. It is safer to treat those as planned extensions rather than features that are already available to everyone.

## How voice and expressive avatars work

The main change is the way people provide input. A typed prompt asks the user to stop, think and write a request. Voice lets a person explain an idea as it develops. They can add details, correct the agent or change direction without rewriting the entire prompt.

The avatar adds a visual signal. When it reacts while speaking, the conversation can feel more continuous. Meta says users can choose a simple voice-only layout or show the avatar beside the conversation. The full-screen option makes the interaction more face-to-face.

The feature also supports interruption. That matters because a spoken conversation should not force a user to wait for a long response before they can correct a misunderstanding. Meta says people can interrupt the agent if they need to. The company has not published a detailed technical description of the interruption system in this announcement.

The voice and avatar are part of a broader move toward AI agents that can work through a task while a person continues the conversation. Meta's examples are everyday tasks rather than a claim that Muse can complete every action without review. Users should still check important details before relying on an answer or a suggested action.

## Why this update matters

Voice changes the way people interact with an assistant. It can be useful when typing is inconvenient, such as while walking, preparing a presentation or thinking through a plan. A natural conversation may also help users explain a vague idea before they know exactly what they want.

Expressive avatars add another layer. They can make a voice response easier to follow because the visual character reacts while speaking. At the same time, a more human-looking interface can make it easier to overestimate what the system understands. A moving avatar is not evidence of human awareness or emotion.

The feature also shows how AI products are competing on the complete interaction, not only on the underlying model. A strong model can answer questions, but the app experience determines how people speak to it, interrupt it, change settings and use it across devices.

For Meta, a voice interface may make Muse more useful as a daily assistant. The company is also linking the experience to its hardware plans. If voice conversations arrive on AI glasses and Muse Charm, people may be able to use the same agent in more situations. That remains a planned direction until Meta confirms those releases.

## What remains unknown

The announcement gives a feature overview, but it does not answer every product question.

Meta has not provided a specific date for the global rollout. It has not published a complete list of supported languages, detailed account eligibility or a full set of technical requirements in the Newsroom post. It also does not provide a detailed breakdown of how voice data is stored, reviewed or used for product improvement in this announcement.

Users should read the current privacy information and settings before using voice with personal or sensitive information. A voice conversation may include details that a person would not normally type into a chat. Do not assume that voice input has a different privacy policy unless the product documentation says so.

The company also has not published independent performance tests for the new voice mode. The Verge's hands-on report is useful as an early observation, but it is not a benchmark across devices or network conditions.

## What users and product teams should watch

For users, the first thing to check is whether the feature is available in the Muse app. If it appears, try a simple task such as brainstorming or planning a short trip. Notice how the agent handles interruptions and corrections. Check the final details rather than treating the spoken answer as automatically correct.

For product teams, the update is another example of voice, visual feedback and task-oriented assistants moving closer together. A useful voice assistant needs more than a natural-sounding response. It must handle interruptions, keep track of context, make its actions clear and provide a way for users to correct it.

The avatar should support the task rather than distract from it. Users need to understand when the assistant is listening, when it is processing and when it is speaking. Clear controls and privacy information matter as much as the visual design.

## Final takeaway

Meta is rolling out voice conversations and expressive avatars for Muse in the United States across mobile, web and Mac. The update makes the assistant easier to use in situations where typing is inconvenient and gives the interaction a more visual style.

The next important details are rollout availability, supported languages, privacy controls and the planned arrival on AI glasses and Muse Charm. Until Meta publishes those details, users should treat the update as a staged app rollout rather than a global release.
