Artlist is not the only place to find music and sound effects for videos. The right alternative depends on how often you publish, whether you work for clients, and whether you need only music or a wider set of creative assets. Epidemic Sound and Soundstripe are useful for regular video production. Envato Elements is broader, PremiumBeat sells individual track licences, and YouTube Audio Library is a free starting point for YouTube videos.

## Quick Answer

Start with Epidemic Sound if you publish often and want a subscription built around music and sound effects. Compare Soundstripe if you need subscription and single-track licensing, especially for commercial creator work. Choose Envato Elements if music is only one part of your production needs and you also download footage, templates, graphics, and fonts. PremiumBeat is useful when you want to buy one track for one defined project instead of keeping another monthly subscription. If you only publish on YouTube and have a small budget, check YouTube Audio Library first.

Do not choose from catalogue size alone. A track can sound perfect and still be the wrong choice if its licence does not cover a client video, paid advertisement, podcast, broadcast, or the platform where you plan to publish. Read the licence before you download and keep proof of permission with the project files.

## How We Chose These Alternatives

This comparison focuses on the practical reasons creators move away from Artlist: payment model, music and sound effect coverage, client work, single-track licensing, and access to other creative assets. We reviewed the current product and licensing information published by each provider. We did not run a sound quality test or claim that one catalogue is the best. Music taste is personal, and search results depend on genre, mood, instrumentation, and the type of video.

Artlist itself has more than one plan. Its Music and SFX Social licence is aimed at personal creators who do not work for clients or brands. Its Pro licence covers commercial work, client projects, advertising, and a wider range of publishing. Artlist says a project completed and published while a subscription is active remains licensed after cancellation, but the assets cannot be used in new projects after the subscription expires. Read the current [Artlist plan guide](https://help.artlist.io/hc/en-us/articles/29559573229725-Artlist-s-Music-and-SFX-plans-explained) and [licence explanation](https://help.artlist.io/hc/en-us/articles/29490991524253-Understanding-Artlist-s-license/) before comparing a cheaper plan with a professional one.

## Best Alternatives

### 1. Epidemic Sound: best for recurring video publishing

Epidemic Sound is a direct alternative when music and sound effects are the main things you need. It offers subscription plans for creators and professional users. The company says its plans include unlimited downloads and usage, copyright protection, and access to plugins. Its pricing page also separates creator use from professional work, so check the plan before you publish a client campaign or paid advertisement.

This service is worth comparing if you publish videos every week, manage several channels, or need a regular supply of music and effects. A subscription can be easier to budget for than buying tracks one by one. However, you still need to match the plan to the work. Do not assume a personal creator plan covers advertising, a client-owned campaign, or a large brand. Review the current [Epidemic Sound plans](https://www.epidemicsound.com/pricing/) and confirm the channel and commercial coverage before you subscribe.

### 2. Soundstripe: best for subscription or single-track licensing

Soundstripe gives creators two ways to pay. You can use an unlimited subscription or buy a single-use licence for one track. Its current pricing guide lists Personal, Pro, and Pro Plus individual plans, with monthly and yearly billing. The Pro plan is intended for professionals who create commercial content for clients. Pro Plus adds access to a large stock video collection.

Soundstripe explains that projects created while a subscription is active stay licensed, while new projects cannot use the downloaded music after the subscription ends. That rule matters if you keep editing old campaigns or reuse tracks in new videos. The provider also offers single-use licence tiers for projects that do not justify a recurring plan. Compare the current [Soundstripe prices](https://www.soundstripe.com/knowledge/plans-coverage/pricing-faq) with its [licence coverage guide](https://www.soundstripe.com/knowledge/licensing-usage/what-can-i-do-with-license/). The plan name alone is not enough. Check whether the project is personal, client-owned, an advertisement, or broadcast work.

### 3. Envato Elements: best for a wider creative asset library

Envato Elements is useful when you need more than music. Its subscription includes stock video, photos, video templates, music, sound effects, design templates, graphics, 3D assets, fonts, and other creative files. That wider mix can be useful for a creator who builds a video from several types of assets and wants one place to search.

The licence is project based. Envato says each download receives an ongoing commercial licence for one specified project use. If you want to use the same item in another project, you need to create a new licence for that use. This differs from treating a download as a general asset that can be reused anywhere. Envato's current [pricing page](https://elements.envato.com/pricing) and [licence guide](https://help.elements.envato.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000628966-Envato-Elements-License) explain the asset range and project rules. It is a better fit for someone who also needs footage and templates than for a person who only wants a few songs.

### 4. PremiumBeat: best for one-off track purchases

PremiumBeat is worth considering when you want to license a specific track for a specific project. Its current page lists single-track licence tiers, including a Creator option for personal web and social projects, a Standard option for commercial web and social work, and wider tiers for non-web advertising and larger professional uses. The displayed US prices are a snapshot and can change, so check the licence page before paying.

A one-time purchase can be easier to manage if you publish only a few videos each year. You do not need to keep a subscription active just to find a track for the next project. The trade-off is that each licence has defined coverage. PremiumBeat's comparison table lists project counts, monetised channel limits, client work, broadcast, and other use cases. Review the current [PremiumBeat licence table](https://www.premiumbeat.com/license) before selecting a tier. A cheaper licence is not a saving if it does not cover the distribution you need.

### 5. YouTube Audio Library: best free option for YouTube-only work

YouTube Audio Library is a sensible first stop when you are building a channel on a small budget. You can find production music and sound effects inside YouTube Studio. YouTube says those assets are copyright-safe for videos on its platform, and creators in the YouTube Partner Program can monetise videos that use them. Some tracks use a Creative Commons licence and require credit in the video description. Other tracks have a standard Audio Library licence that does not require attribution.

The main limitation is scope. Do not assume a track from YouTube Audio Library is automatically cleared for an advertisement, podcast, client project, or video published on another service. YouTube says the library is available through YouTube Studio and that it cannot give legal guidance about off-platform use. Read the [YouTube Audio Library instructions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882) and check the licence shown for each track.

## How to Choose

Write down the next three projects you expect to publish. For each one, record who owns the video, where it will appear, whether it is monetised, and whether it promotes a product or service. Then choose the licence that covers all those details. If you make client work, ask whether the client needs to edit or reuse the music themselves. Some licences let you deliver a finished video but do not let you hand over the raw music file or the right to use it in a separate project.

Next, compare how you search. A library with a strong search tool can save time when you need a particular mood or tempo. Make a shortlist using the same brief, such as a calm product video, a fast tutorial, and a cinematic opening. Check whether the service offers stems, sound effects, filters, or editing integrations that match your workflow. These features matter more when you produce regularly.

Finally, calculate the real cost. A yearly subscription may be cheaper per month but costs more upfront. A single-track licence may be better if you publish only a few projects. A broad creative subscription may be good value if you also need footage and templates, but it is unnecessary if you only use music. Compare the full cost with your actual download needs rather than the catalogue's advertised size.

## What to Avoid

Do not treat the words royalty-free as a promise that a track is free or unrestricted. It usually describes how royalties are handled after you obtain the correct licence. Do not download a track on a personal plan and then place it in a client's paid campaign without checking the terms. Do not share account credentials or send raw licensed files to a client if the agreement does not allow it.

Keep a copy of the licence, receipt, project name, track title, and download date. Save the document with the project so another editor can confirm why the music was used. If a subscription ends, do not assume that an old download can be used in a new video. Check the provider's rules for existing projects, new projects, channel registration, and client handover.

## Key Takeaways

- Save licence proof and check the terms before cancelling.

## FAQ

### What is the best alternative to Artlist?

There is no universal winner. Epidemic Sound and Soundstripe are useful for recurring video publishing. Envato Elements is broader, PremiumBeat offers one-time licences, and YouTube Audio Library is free for eligible YouTube use. The right option depends on the licence you need.

### Which Artlist alternative is best for YouTube creators?

Compare Epidemic Sound, Soundstripe, and YouTube Audio Library. Check how many channels are covered, whether monetisation is allowed, whether attribution is required, and whether the licence covers other platforms. A low price does not help if the licence excludes your channel.

### Can I use royalty-free music in client videos?

Yes, if the licence covers client work and the intended distribution. A personal or social creator licence may not cover paid advertising, branded content, or a project owned by a client. Read the current terms before you download the track.

### Is YouTube Audio Library free?

YouTube provides music and sound effects in its Audio Library at no charge for use in videos on YouTube. Some tracks require attribution. Check the licence column for each track before publishing and do not assume the same permission covers a podcast or a client project elsewhere.

### Does a music subscription protect old videos after cancellation?

Some services keep a project licensed when it was created and published while the subscription was active. Other rules may apply to new edits or new projects, so save proof and read the provider's terms. Keep the licence with the project archive.

### Can I use one music licence on every platform?

Not always. Some plans cover selected social channels, while others cover client work, ads, podcasts, or broadcast. Check the licence for each project and platform before publishing. If the use is unclear, ask the provider before using the track.
