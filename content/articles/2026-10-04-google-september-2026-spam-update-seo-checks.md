Google’s September 2026 spam update is a good reason for SEOs to slow down before reacting to ranking changes. Google says the update started on September 24, 2026, applies globally and to all languages, and may take up to two weeks to roll out. The Search Status Dashboard still listed the update as active on October 3.

That means a traffic change seen during this window is not enough evidence to justify a large sitewide rewrite.

## What Happened?

Google released the September 2026 spam update at 09:15 PDT on September 24. The official status page identifies it as a Ranking incident and says the rollout can take up to two weeks.

Google’s spam-update documentation explains that these updates are improvements to automated spam-detection systems. Google also describes SpamBrain as an AI-based spam-prevention system.

A spam update is not the same thing as a core update. It is focused on systems that detect spam and abusive behavior. Google says sites that violate spam policies may rank lower or not appear in results.

## Why People Are Talking About It

SEO communities often react to an update by looking for one tactic that explains every ranking movement. That is risky during a live rollout.

A Reddit discussion about the September update described the update as global and raised questions about its rollout window. The thread is useful for understanding reader interest, but it is not proof that a particular site changed because of the update.

The better approach is to separate three things: timing, affected pages and affected search behavior.

If only a few pages move, the cause may be page-specific. If many pages using the same template or content pattern move together, that pattern deserves investigation. If branded queries stay stable while generic queries fall, the problem may be different again.

## What Users Experienced

The most useful signals are usually in Search Console and your own analytics.

Compare the seven days before September 24 with the same number of days after it. Then compare affected pages with pages that did not move. Look at clicks, impressions, average position and query groups.

Do not use clicks alone. A page can lose clicks because search demand changed, because another result became more attractive, or because a search feature reduced traditional clicks. A ranking change is stronger evidence when impressions and average position move together.

Also check whether the change is limited to one country, device type, template, folder or content format.

Look at landing-page behavior as well. If organic traffic falls but conversions remain stable, the business impact may be smaller than the traffic chart suggests. If both traffic and qualified leads fall, the diagnosis deserves more attention.

## Why It Happens

Google’s spam policies cover techniques that manipulate search systems or deceive users. They include areas such as scaled content abuse, link spam, cloaking and other forms of search manipulation.

Google’s guidance on generative AI is especially relevant for publishers using large-scale content workflows. Google says generative AI can be useful for research and structure, but generating many pages without adding value may violate its policy on scaled content abuse.

That does not mean AI-assisted content is automatically spam. The key question is whether the publishing process creates useful pages for people or mainly creates pages to influence rankings.

This is why a sitewide decision such as deleting hundreds of pages should wait until you know what changed.

## Working Fixes

First, audit pages that lost visibility rather than changing everything.

Check whether affected pages answer search intent clearly. Look for copied or near-duplicate sections, weak original information, excessive templated text, aggressive keyword use and pages created mainly to capture search traffic.

Then inspect internal links. Important pages should be reachable through sensible navigation and contextual links. Avoid building large blocks of artificial internal links simply because an update is running.

Review external links too. If the site recently bought links, published large guest-post campaigns or gained unusual backlink patterns, document those changes before deciding what to do.

For AI-assisted content, compare the pages against Google’s own guidance. Ask whether each page contains original information, useful examples, clear ownership where relevant, and a reason for existing beyond search traffic.

Finally, wait for the rollout to finish before judging the full effect. A live rollout can make short-term measurements noisy.

## What Doesn’t Work

Do not respond to a live spam update by changing titles, deleting pages, changing URLs and rebuilding the site at the same time. You will lose the ability to identify what helped or hurt.

Do not assume a traffic drop proves a manual action. Manual actions and algorithmic ranking systems are different. Check Search Console for an actual message instead of guessing.

Do not buy backlinks to recover rankings. If the update is related to spam systems, adding more questionable signals can make the situation harder to understand.

Do not copy a competitor’s content pattern just because it ranks. Their site has different history, links, users and signals.

Do not rewrite every page simply to make it longer. Word count is not a substitute for original information or a useful answer.

## Official Response

Google’s official Search Status Dashboard is the best source for rollout status. It records the September 2026 spam update as beginning on September 24 and says the rollout may take up to two weeks.

Google’s spam-update documentation says sites affected after a spam update should review the spam policies. It also notes that improvements can take time to be recognized by automated systems.

Google’s Search Central guidance on generative AI also makes an important distinction: using AI as a tool is not the same as using generated content at scale without adding value.

## Key Takeaways

- Google’s September 2026 spam update began on September 24.
- Google says the rollout is global and can take up to two weeks.
- A ranking change is not proof of a spam-policy violation.
- Use Search Console to compare affected and unaffected pages.
- Review AI-assisted publishing for scaled-content risks.
- Avoid major sitewide changes until the rollout and diagnosis are clear.

The safest workflow is simple: measure, isolate, inspect, improve and then wait for Google to recrawl and reassess the changes.

A practical audit can also be split into three buckets. Bucket one contains obvious policy or quality problems that should be fixed now. Bucket two contains pages with traffic changes but no clear quality issue; these need monitoring. Bucket three contains pages that improved, because they can reveal patterns worth learning from.

Do not ignore the winners. If pages with first-hand examples, stronger internal links, clearer authorship or better task completion improved while similar pages fell, those differences can guide future work without copying a competitor or chasing a temporary ranking trick.

The update should therefore be treated as a diagnostic event, not a reason to panic. Good SEO decisions come from controlled comparisons and evidence.

## FAQ

**Is Google’s September 2026 spam update finished?**

The Search Status Dashboard still showed it as active on October 3, 2026, so SEOs should check the live dashboard before treating the rollout as complete.

**Does the spam update target AI content?**

Not simply because content uses AI. Google’s guidance focuses on whether content is created at scale without adding value or is used to manipulate search rankings.

**Should I delete pages after a traffic drop?**

Not automatically. First identify whether the pages have common quality, spam, technical or search-intent issues.

**How should I measure the impact?**

Compare affected pages, queries, countries and devices before and after September 24. Use clicks, impressions and average position together.

**Can a spam update cause a manual action?**

A ranking-system update and a manual action are different systems. Check Search Console for an actual manual-action notice.

**When should I make changes?**

Fix clear problems as soon as you identify them, but avoid making many unrelated changes while the rollout is still active.
